## 1. What the chapter does

It tells how the law changes. Article VII says that the law grows only from evidence, and the chapter reads the law's own history
as data: the lineage inside `constitution/rules.json`, entry by entry, the docket where each change was proposed and ruled, the
harvest that brought Rules 117 to 124, and the numbers that were retired and burned. It tells three cases from the record: a
correction that turned out to be law already (and the one new rule the episode left, Rule 129), two rules that contradicted each
other and how both were amended at version 3.11 with no rule of precedence, and a check that had run without a rule until
Rule 130 was written for it at version 3.12. It shows what an amendment costs: the contract, the fixtures, the pins and the
shipped files that move with one rule.

## 8. Where the field leads

The win is section 7's to state once read. The loss, from the record's own sentences on what amendment from evidence costs the
law itself:

- A law fed by single cases can swell: "Three new numbers from one surface is a lot, and the risk is corpus inflation."
  (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:582`)
- The evidence is often one case: "The evidence is one surface, one operator, one implementer." (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:596`);
  "A rule canonized from a single platform's latency is exactly the kind of over-generalisation Article VII exists to prevent."
  (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:430-432`)
- A law that grows rule by rule can contradict itself: "two rules can still contradict each other, and the only reason this pair
  was caught is that someone put them side by side by hand" (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:756-758`)
- A short prohibition loses to habit: "Rule 95's `avoid` entry is four words against the single most common answer on the open
  web, and four words lost" (`doc/EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md:202`)
- Everything that states the law's version can fall behind it: "a number in prose is attached to nothing that can contradict it"
  (`doc/EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md:252`); M9 (`:226`)
- A gap can be seen and left open for want of evidence: "Rule 116 forbids one page under two names and nothing forbids one
  subject under two pages, which is a real gap" (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:852`)
- And beside them the win in the record's words: "The constitution's value is highest exactly where it contradicts the industry
  default" (`doc/EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md:202`); "Retired numbers are never reused. Rule 79 means the same thing in ten years"
  (`protocol/README.md:103`).

## 13. Hazards

- **Names.** The lineage (`rules.json:10`), the harvest, the docket and the ledger name the language's two former names, its
  family name, the law's first home, a sibling language's site, a product site, a banking product, the coordinating session and
  components of the first book's library. None goes on a page. The lineage cannot be printed whole, and Appendix A ("The
  Constitution in full") needs a ruling on `metadata.lineage` and on the evidence fields before it is generated (finding 14).
- **The author's words are recorded with their typing.** Quote exactly or not at all; "spelling as recorded" is a choice for the
  author (three quotations above carry it). Under 25 words each, with path and line. The record itself holds one of them in two
  spellings (section 10): name the file quoted.
- **Who ruled.** Never "the author ruled" for 2026-08-16 (a delegation), 2026-11-02 (finding 2), 2026-11-03 (his word to close
  the points; two of its items apply his own rulings of 2026-10-16), nor for the second pass (unnamed). "The record says" is the
  safe form.
- **No tier count.** The docket's arithmetic lines carry tier censuses and a percentage (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:92, 108-111,
  569-570`; ledger A1, A5, L8). None is proposed here and none is printed before the author's ruling (`research/REQUESTS.md`).
  A rule's tier is given for that rule only, as the file's word.
- **Versions.** Say which thing a version belongs to each time: the law (3.x), the language (1.x), the contract (1.x), the
  command (0.2.0). `FIELD_MISPLACED` is a change of the language with no version of the law.
- **"Before" is told, "after" is run.** The pin holds one verifier. A sentence such as "this was lawful at 1.3" rests on
  `doc/language/README.md:17-18`, not on a run.
- **`unverified` in the ledger** (E5 on safety standards, I1 on road signage "amended on crash evidence") is not printed as
  fact; docket C-2 gates E5. Sections F to I are `reported` and belong to the field, to be read at their sources.
- **The field is unread.** Section 7 holds no fact. No sentence on WCAG's versions, on a platform's change log or on semantic
  versioning is drafted before `research/FIELD.md` has the reading and its date.
- **Stage, never weakness.** The record's own phrases ("Not built", "nothing checks", "nobody had") are evidence, cited in this
  file; on the page they become what exists and the stage the rest has reached.
- **A block with a silent extra finding.** Block 3h without its `ROLE` line gives a warning; a level 3 plate needs
  `level=3 symbols=<name.json>` and its `symbols` fence.
- **The record's sentences are not prose.** Several are quotable and tempting ("a proposed number is not a free number", "noted
  rather than smoothed"): quote with path, or say it in the book's words.
- Outside the Zayn record, for the writer: the house's rule HOUSE-20 (`../../CLAUDE.md`, "Menus") is this same amendment arriving
  in the book's own reader. It is the house's document, not evidence of the pin.

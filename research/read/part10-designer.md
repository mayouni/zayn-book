# Part X, In Practice: read from the designer's door

The designer who does not program. Two pages, read in order, twice: chapter 25, then chapter 26 (the book's last).

## Chapter 25, The command and the gate

1. **What I remember.** A gate reads one number: 1 when a declaration is refused (the build stops), 0 when none is, warnings
   included, 2 when the command could not read its file. A team chooses the level, the table and the files; at level 3 without
   the table the check is silent, and with it each file is judged alone against the whole table.

2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "The command, as released", the release table: "aarch64-linux", "SHA-256", ".wasm" are never said; "site/zayn.wasm, which
     the site runs" means nothing to me.
   - Used before said: "verbs and options" (opening; said a section later); "the suite of fixtures" (said in The court); "pins"
     (said in What I refused); "the contract at 1.11" (said in What a team chooses). Never said: "Node", "protected branch",
     "merge", "static analysis", "the artifact is unlawful", "operator" (the verifier's word where the page says user), "compares
     two media".
   - Two contracts: I took "the family's Diagnostic Contract" and "the file that binds each kind of finding to the law" for one
     thing until the run box printed "violations.json v1.11" and "diagnostic-contract v1.2.2".
   - What I refused, the pack: content-addressed, pins, manifest, vendor, tree, five words said in one paragraph. I lost it there.

3. **Where I doubted.**
   - The opening promises "every other word is said where it first appears"; the list above breaks it.
   - "undecidable is silence, never a pass" stands beside a run that prints "clean" and answers 0, which the gate reads as go.
     For my build it is a pass.
   - "the verifier reads one file at a time; the next section shows what that means": that section never runs entry.zayn with
     the flow's file, so I do not know whether the warning goes.
   - The run box prints "59 diagnostics" and "40 diagnostics"; nothing says why two counts.

4. **What was mine.** Where my judgment stays once a gate exists. Plate 25.11 gives it: "final" passes as "reversible" does, and
   the page asks "Who reads that difference before the change ships?" I got it, as a question. Exercise 25.9 was fair: the hint
   on "reversible" and the lawful select-confirm-act flows let me find the three faults, if not the codes' names. The stake is a
   visible "[AUTHOR: ...]" line, so the why for a team is missing.

5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** I can restate the three numbers and the level 3 trap; the release table and the
   pack serve another door, and the two-file section leaves me no action, since the build has stopped at 1. Stopped most:
   "keeps a surface and the state it shows in one file, or reads the refusal with that in mind."

## Chapter 26, A site under its own law

1. **What I remember.** A site that publishes a law declares its menu and pages, runs them, checks its files and audits its
   painted page in a browser; each instrument decides only what it can see. Proof: Maximise said as an act is refused, said as
   focus is clean, while K36, moved from the popup to the editor inside it, now reads the 935 pixels of 1596 no declaration sees.

2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - The title line carries "Spine", never said.
   - The principle: "I had asked for that register of writing more than once": which register? Named only in What I refused.
   - A remark, a check: "The corpus keeps each remark of mine with its date": corpus never said.
   - Three instruments: "Milestone 2 is the second step of the site's design system": a design system has no steps; I read
     "redesign".
   - Plate 26.5: "One of its pages is left out": I cannot find which in the plate.
   - The court: "Of one fact of the painted page": which fact?
   - Where it stands: "where checks that count their cases today begin to fail".
   - Plate 26.3 cites "A Card Is Not a Button" and "Motion Reports, It Never Decorates" for a Maximise button; nothing says why.

3. **Where I doubted.**
   - The principle quotes "hides its own errors has refuted itself"; the field's GOV.UK "keeps its failures in a public list";
     then What I refused: "A public page that lists what the project lacks." The page never separates a failure found from a
     thing not built, so I read it as hiding, and the quoted "hurts the project image (negative markeing)!" deepens that.
   - K37: "the code's pane lost 149 and the report's gained 150", told as success; the missing pixel is not explained.
   - Plate 26.1 lists three families its file never declares, and runs clean.

4. **What was mine.** The line between what a declaration says and what the painted page shows. Got it whole: "how large the
   editor is drawn belongs to the painted page, and K36 reads it." I could sort my four remarks by instrument without looking.
   Exercise 26.6, a clean file with a real defect, taught the same line from the other side.

5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The chain of remarks is the part's clearest account of a designer's remark becoming
   a check; what stops me is local terms and the tension on public failures. Stopped most: "A public page that lists what the
   project lacks."

## The part

**Hand-over.** Chapter 25 ends "The last chapter runs it on the Zayn site's own declarations"; chapter 26 opens by saying what
such a site is, and hands over to Appendix A. As the book's last page, it ends on a pointer and an "[AUTHOR: ...]" line in place
of the stake: the book closes without its author's why.

**Where the pages disagree.**
- Court: "calls each verifier of the family a court" (25, What I refused) against "The court of this chapter is these fixtures
  of the suite" (25 and 26).
- A missing name: chapter 25 warns, "An INTENT_FLOW reference names no declared flow"; Plate 26.1 lists "build_family,
  web_family, audit_family", declared nowhere, and is clean.
- Level 4: chapter 25, "Level 4 is the painted page" and "conforms at level 4"; chapter 26, "Undecidable from a declaration: it
  is a fact about a painted page." What does level 4 decide?

**Clearest plate:** 26.4, read with 26.3 and the 935 of 1596. **Hardest:** 25.8 and its two-file command line.

**What I take away.** A refusal stops a build, a warning does not, and a gate without its table is silent at level 3. A
declaration states what a control does, never how large it is drawn; the painted page has its own checks, each written to fail
first. And a person still reads what a control does against what it says.

# Part IV, The Expression: read from the designer's door

Reader: the designer who does not program. Pages read twice, in order, from `.cache/as-read/` (chapters 10 and 11). 2026-11-27.

## Chapter 10, What a declaration cannot see

1. **What I remember.** One declaration can sit under a careful drawing and under one with seven faults, and the verifier passes both, because those faults belong to the drawing. Rules split in two: the verifier refuses a missing undo (Plate 10.3), and an audit of the painted page measures most of the drawing's half, with a person judging the rest.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - Opening: "carrying the seven faults the record names": "the record" comes before it is said; "the ledger" is defined only later.
   - One declaration, two drawings: "a check of level 3 also needs a symbol table, the list of what the system knows". I could not tell what levels are, or why level 4 still gives a clean answer.
   - Same section: "Chapter 4 explains the run; here it shows the line." I could not tell what "it" is.
   - What reads the drawing: "in both of the site's cultures, the two ways it draws the same interface". Two languages? Two directions of text? I could not tell.
   - What I refused: "The column does not; a surface may express an obligation any way it likes." The column does not do what? The quote starts in mid-thought.
   - Terms used before they are said: TONE (in "a TONE on a SCREEN"), "enforcement ladder", "a renderer that Softanza owns" (Softanza is never said), K22 (named as the measure of LAYOUT_DISPLACEMENT, but the table files K22 under "the page in time").
3. **Where I doubted.** "The law is Zayn's constitution of 123 rules", yet the page cites Rule 129. How can a law of 123 rules have a Rule 129? "Five of the seven faults meet a check": I had to build the pairing myself (K19 for "Invalid input!", K03 for the wall of text). A small table, fault beside check, would prove the claim.
4. **What was mine.** The two drawings of Plate 10.1 and 10.2, and the list of checks grouped by what they read. I got both. After the page I could say which of my design review's faults a machine reads and which stay mine. The closing question, "how will the drawing show it?", speaks to my trade.
5. **Verdict:** ready after the items above. The page's claim is clear and its drawings carry it. The levels paragraph is the one place I stopped. Sentence that stopped me most: "The verifier holds none, so at level 4 it runs the checks it holds".

## Chapter 11, The strata of expression

1. **What I remember.** Between what a user does and how a medium draws it sits a middle layer of six functions: structure, signal, role, motion, message and tempo. Each names a meaning, never a look. SIGNALS and ROLE are built and refuse a colour ("red") or a widget ("button") where a meaning or a promise belongs. Motion must report a state, and tempo lives in the law.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - Signal: "five statuses, each with the rule's own meaning". The page names two (danger, warning). I wanted all five, as a designer chooses from them.
   - In your hands: "a signal's word where a tone goes". The page never gives TONE's list, and Plate 11.10 repairs `danger` to `critical`. I could not predict the repair: the exercise needs chapter 7 now, not as a forward pointer.
   - What I refused: "the law's layer holds the vocabulary of interfaces as data". "Reference design" and "layer" are not said.
   - Tempo: "Rules 81 and 91 hold the wait and business time". I could not tell what "business time" is.
   - The court: UNKNOWN_SIGNAL is "level 1", while every report says "at level 2". Levels are not explained (as in chapter 10).
3. **Where I doubted.**
   - The court lists ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN, "A reversible control keeps no reachable UNDO", but no plate on the page runs it. And chapter 10's Plate 10.3 is a reversible control with no UNDO that got only MISSING_UNDO. Why not this one too? See "for the part".
   - "Each one built refuses what it names" closes a page whose table says tempo is "designed in the language". The sentence reads larger than the table.
   - Again "123 rules" beside Rule 125.
4. **What was mine.** "Expression is not appearance", and the signal plates: I name the meaning, and each medium draws it. That is the brief I give a design system, and I got it. The strata table in "Where it stands" is the page I would pin to the wall.
5. **Verdict:** ready after the items above. The exercise is the one place my door fails: it asks me to repair a tone whose list I was never shown. Sentence that stopped me most: "a signal's word where a tone goes".

## For the part

- **Hand-over.** Chapter 10 ends by naming The Second Founding and "words for them", and chapter 11 opens with them. Chapter 11 hands over to Part V by its channels. Both hand-overs hold.
- **Contradictions.**
  - Chapter 10, Plate 10.3: `ROLE reversible`, no UNDO, gives only "MISSING_UNDO approve_payment". Chapter 11, the court: "ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN ... A reversible control keeps no reachable UNDO." To a reader, both should have fired on 10.3. One sentence should say why only one did.
  - Chapter 11 uses `danger` as a lawful SIGNAL (Plate 11.2) and refuses it as a TONE (Plate 11.9). This is lawful, but on these pages it reads as a contradiction until chapter 7 is read.
  - "123 rules" (both openings) beside Rules 125 and 129.
- **Clearest plate:** 10.2, the same file under the faulty drawing, with each fault tagged by its rule. **Hardest:** 11.9, the purge, for the missing TONE list. The level 4 command line in chapter 10 is the hardest passage that is not a plate.
- **What I take away.** I can now sort a design review's notes into three piles: what the declaration states and the verifier refuses (an undo exists, a promise is honest, a signal is a meaning), what the audit measures on the painted page (size, contrast, colours, line length), and what stays with a person (competing buttons, how far the undo sits, whether a shake fits). I could restate the six strata, and which are built, without looking.

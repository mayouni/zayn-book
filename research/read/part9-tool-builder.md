## The part

**Hand-overs.** Each page hands over to the next: 22 ends "the next chapter takes up that side"; 23 ends "The next chapter reads the grammar"; 24 ends "Part X starts there".

**Contradictions.**
- Ch24, Where the field leads: "chapter 22 shows the loop in which one does". Ch22: "The Zayn site describes a loop", which it files as designed. No AI model runs in chapter 22.
- Ch22: the grammar is "a file of rules that an AI model can be held to while it writes a declaration". Ch24: "The grammar accepts one form of the screen, the verifier the other."
- Ch22 and 23 define two levels; ch24 speaks of "four levels".

**Clearest plate.** 22.5: the report for a person, then the machine form, with every field I would emit.
**Hardest.** 24.8: it asks me to predict twenty-four syntax errors that the suite does not pin.

**What I take away.** From the plates and the contract lines I could write a second verifier for every check these pages run, emit the machine form, and choose my own way of recovering from syntax errors. Three things are missing: what the manifest compares (codes alone, or lines and subjects too), how a level and a symbol table reach the command, and what levels 1 and 4 hold. Those three are the conformance contract; the rest is precise enough to build from.

# Part II, The Heritage: read from the door of the designer who does not program (second reader)

Read: chapters 3, 4 and 5, in that order, twice, from `.cache/as-read/`. Nothing else.

## Chapter 3, What the field wrote

1. **What I remember.** Nielsen, Shneiderman, the two gulfs and Tufte map onto numbered rules, and where a declaration can state the heuristic (undo, confirm, visible state, a status word) the verifier refuses by name. Proof: four offending plates and their repairs; size, choices and grouping stay with a person on the painted page.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "The State the user must see": "level 2 adds the checks the file alone decides". I could not say what that means.
   - Plate 3.2: a hidden filter passes "clean" without the table. Why silence is acceptable is deferred to chapter 25; I needed one sentence now.
   - Plate 3.8: "The law files Rule 118 under judgment, and the refusal judges a word". Tier against refusal confused me until "The map" explained it 80 lines later.
   - "Where the field leads": "the same byte for byte" and "clean at level 4"; level 4 is not said in this chapter.
   - "Where it stands": "expression fields proposed in the second founding" in the short lines; the prose defines it only after.
   - The court box says UNKNOWN_SIGNAL is "level 1", Plate 3.8 reports "at level 2". Unexplained.
3. **Where I doubted.** Plate 3.6 cites Rule 16, whose words are "clear visual affordance"; the page owns the gap ("finds it in the article above the rule"), which helps, but a designer reading the report alone would be misled.
4. **What was mine.** The map table: I could restate that Nielsen's undo is Rule 79 and that Fitts belongs to the painted page. Got it.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The sentence that stopped me most: "level 2 adds the checks the file alone decides".

## Chapter 4, The instruments of the field

1. **What I remember.** Tokens, stories, linters, screenshots and layout-shift scores each read one object of the drawing; a declaration reads what the user is doing, so two cards drawn alike can be one lawful and one refused. Proof: the plan picker and the two dashboard cards, then a clean notice whose layout shift only the audit can measure.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "The field": "the impact fraction of D multiplied by the distance fraction of D". D is never said; "CLS" is never spelt out.
   - Plate 4.3: the finding cites Rule 112, "Motion Reports", for a card. The reason given ("inert informs and navigate changes what is shown") did not tell me why motion.
   - "Clean, and the drawing still owes": level 4 "requires a rendered surface", yet the run at level 4 "reads the declaration". I could not hold both.
   - "K17 TARGET", "K22 LAYOUT_STILL": codes with no sentence for me.
   - "The law names its instruments": "declaration lint", "manifest lint", "html lint", "graphics plane", "head" in one paragraph. Here I stopped understanding.
   - "What I refused": "an issue that is statically checked" ("statically" is not said).
   - The closing: "The first finding's message says 'change nothing'". In Plate 4.6 that message is the third finding, not the first.
3. **Where I doubted.** "only a declaration says which" (two surfaces, same tokens): a brief or a product spec also says which. The claim is larger than the page shows; "a declaration says it in a form a machine checks" would hold.
4. **What was mine.** Design tokens and Storybook, held against intent. Plate 4.3 and 4.4 gave it to me: "one drawing and two promises". Got it, and it is the best idea in the part for my door.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The sentence that stopped me most: "it names a manifest lint, owed to read the profile a project's manifest names".

## Chapter 5, The road sign

1. **What I remember.** The Vienna Convention fixes a closed set of signs so a stranger reads them at speed, and Zayn closes its lists the same way: media, verbs a medium carries, five status meanings. Proof: a lobby sign cannot open a gate, a placard cannot ask to be read but a printed notice can, and "road_sign" and "critical" are refused as words outside the lists.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "A sign in Zayn's data": verbs carried "natively, at a cost, or not at all". "At a cost" is never said.
   - After Plate 5.1: "The article on a finding's line is the diagnostic's own, set in the contract". Rule 82 shows Article III in chapter 3 and Article II here; the page explains it, but in a sentence I read three times.
   - "Where the field leads": "its edition is pinned by hash"; the six properties ("seriality, bandwidth, mutability, retractability") are listed with no word for each.
3. **Where I doubted.** The page treats the record's "most successful interface standard in human history" well: it checks the line at its source and restates it at its size. No doubt left. The exercise's twist (an exit sign cannot carry EXIT) first felt like a trick; the closing sentence settled it.
4. **What was mine.** A precedent a design review knows: a vocabulary closed by law. Got it, and Plates 5.3 and 5.4 made it concrete.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The sentence that stopped me most: "The article on a finding's line is the diagnostic's own, set in the contract".

  - Chapter 4 calls the ROLE finding "the first finding"; the report lists it third.
- **Repetition.** The paragraph "If you open the book here, four words come first" opens all three chapters word for word; read in order, it is read three times.
- **Clearest plate:** 5.3 with 5.4, the same declaration refused on a sign and clean on print: one verb, one medium.
- **Hardest plate:** 4.3, a card refused under a rule about motion.
- **What I take away.** The field gave me advice and instruments that read the drawing; Zayn adds a declaration of what the user does, and refuses a wrong intent before anything is drawn, while size, grouping and layout stay with the audit and with me.

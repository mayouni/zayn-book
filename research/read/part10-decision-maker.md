# Part X, *In Practice*, from the decision-maker's door

Reader: the decision-maker who commissions software. Chapters 25 and 26 (the part has two pages), each read once without notes,
then with the brief's questions. 2026-11-24.

## Chapter 25: The command and the gate

**1. What I remember.** A gate reads one number: 1 when the verifier refuses, 0 when it refuses nothing (warnings pass), 2 when
it could not read its input. The level decides what stops: the week clean at level 2 is refused twice at level 3 with the
project's table, and passes silently at level 3 without one.

**2. Where I lost the thread.**
- What a team chooses: "The two files get the same answer." One file was clean, the other refused; I read it twice to see that
  each keeps its own answer.
- Same place: "or reads the refusal with that in mind." A refusal stops the build; reading it does not restart it.
- Where it stands: "designed the gate's step and the pack's check as steps of a team's build". The chapter runs the gate
  throughout; I cannot tell what of it is left to build.
- The court's run box: "59 diagnostics", then "40 diagnostics" one line down, unexplained.
- Terms before they are said: "suite of fixtures" (The command, as released; said in The court); "the contract at 1.11" (said
  under What a team chooses); "Zayn's family" (The principle; never said: is there a second verifier I could choose?); "two
  media" (Where the field leads; never said).

**3. Where I doubted.** The principle adopts the guide's "That is all a CI gate needs." The same page shows that a level-3 gate
also needs its table, and one file per surface and state: the line claims more than the page proves.

**4. What was mine.** A clause for a supplier's contract. I got most of it: "zayn check specs/*.zayn" as a required step at a
stated level; "--symbols" at level 3, since "undecidable is silence, never a pass"; the pack's files vendored, with "zayn pack
--check" in the build. Plate 25.11 gave me the limit: declare the mark irreversible and the gate passes; only "a person on the
team knows what the mark does". Not got: the cost of adoption (the table, "A team writes it today"; the declarations, by whom
and in what time?); and whether a supplier can loosen the gate with "--contract". The pack's hash check would stop that; the page
names both and never joins them, and that join is my clause. Only the Windows file is "built and run"; my supplier's build
service may run Linux.

**5. Verdict: ready after the items above.** The exit codes and the table rule I can use as written; a lawful project split over
two files leaves a level-3 gate stopped, with no stated way through. Stopped me most: "A team that gates at level 3 keeps a
surface and the state it shows in one file".

## Chapter 26: A site under its own law

**1. What I remember.** The Zayn site declares its menu and eight pages, its build reruns the 37 reports its pages print, and a
browser audit measures what is drawn; each of the author's remarks became a check written to fail first. The proof: Maximise is
clean as a declaration while the editor filled 935 pixels of 1596, and the check meant to catch it measured the popup.

**2. Where I lost the thread.**
- A remark, a check: "the side lists, the lists at the side of a page of its family and its headings": I could not parse it.
- Before Plate 26.5: "One of its pages is left out": left out for space, or is that the defect?
- Plate 26.3 cites "Rule 112 · Motion Reports, It Never Decorates" for a button that enlarges an editor; the page does not say why.
- Terms the page's door leaves out: "the record" (from "told from the record" on); "the corpus" (same section); "IDE" ("the
  IDE's own box"; "editor" elsewhere).

**3. Where I doubted.** What I refused: the site refuses "a phrase that lists what the project lacks". The field's GOV.UK keeps
"the failures it finds in a public list", and to a commissioner a list of known failures is evidence. The principle says the
ledger "keeps its own errors in view"; said again here, it would answer me. The quoted remark prints "negative markeing" with no
mark that the spelling is the record's.

**4. What was mine.** What the verifier does not cover, and what does. I got it: three plates show a clean declaration over a real
defect (the editor's size, a side list's level, two pages no menu reaches), and the page names each instrument: the painted-page
audit, the build's checks against the registry, a person ("the Principal looks at it in both cultures"). A check written to fail
first, for each remark, is a practice I can require. Not got: whether my supplier can run that audit on my product.

**5. Verdict: ready after the items above.** Stopped me most: "They are not part of the standard; they are this site's build."
The instrument that caught the real defect is one I cannot require, and the page does not say what I require in its place.

## The part

**Hand-over.** Chapter 25 closes on "Who reads that difference before the change ships?"; chapter 26 answers it in practice: a
browser audit and a person. Chapter 26 ends the book on a question to the Zayn project and Appendix A. No page of *In Practice*
gathers what a commissioner requires; I assembled it from five sections.

**Disagreements.**
- Chapter 25: "Level 4 is the painted page, the page as a browser draws it", and the suite "conforms at level 4" under Node.
  Chapter 26: "Undecidable from a declaration: it is a fact about a painted page"; "The checks K34 to K37 run in a browser".
  Which instrument holds level 4, the verifier or the audit?
- "Verb": "A verb is the first word after the command's name" (25); "FOCUS, ACT and UNDO are three of the law's verbs" (26).

**Clearest plate:** 25.5 with the command line after it, refused twice with the table and clean without. I could restate the rule
without looking: at level 3, the table goes on the same command line.

**Hardest plate:** 26.5: what was left out, what was wrong, and why it is clean stayed open until its last sentence sent me to K34.

**What I take away.** I can require today: declarations checked by the zayn command as a required build step at a stated level,
the table at level 3, the pack pinned by hash. The gate does not see whether a declaration is true of the product, the drawn
page, or a page no menu reaches. Three things I still lack: the cost of adoption, how a project split over files passes level 3,
and what checks the painted page of my own product.

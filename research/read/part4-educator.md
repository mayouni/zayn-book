# Part IV, The Expression: read from the educator's door

Reader: the educator. Pages: chapter 10, then chapter 11, as built (`.cache/as-read/`). Read once without notes, then again with the brief's questions. Date of the reading: 2026-11-27.

## Chapter 10, What a declaration cannot see

1. **What I remember.** One declaration stands clean under a careful drawing and under a drawing with seven faults, because a declaration names no widget, colour or size. A rule such as 79 or 119 splits: the verifier refuses the declaration's half (no undo; a card that lies about its act), and the audit of the painted page and a person judge the drawing's half.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - Plate 10.1: the report says "0 violations at level 2" before "A level is a stage of what a check needs to read" (under Plate 10.2). A student meets the word first in a report.
   - "One rule, two halves": "Article IV · the right to reverse" in the report; articles and rights are not said on this page.
   - Same section: "in a toast for a few seconds": "toast" is not said in a clause.
   - "What I refused": "The contract's note on the finding" and "a TONE on a SCREEN": the contract, TONE and SCREEN are not said in chapter 10 (chapter 11 says the contract).
   - "The line moves": "Rule 129, The Action Is the Last Thing to Go". The door paragraph says the law has "123 rules". A student asks how Rule 129 exists. Same for Rule 125 in chapter 11.
   - "Where it stands": "rules moved up the enforcement ladder": the ladder is not said here (chapter 17).
3. **Where I doubted.**
   - Under Designed: "LAYOUT_DISPLACEMENT, measured by the audit's K22". If the audit measures it, why is it Designed? A student cannot tell what is run today and what is planned.
   - "Five of the seven faults meet a check" lists "K02 TEXT_CONTRAST its contrast", and contrast is not one of the seven faults.
   - "Where it stands" says the same Built/Designed/Next twice, first as a list, then as a paragraph. A student reads it twice and looks for a difference.
4. **What was mine.** Could a student follow this, and is the exercise real? Mostly yes. The order works: a clean pair, then the split of one rule, refused before repaired. The exercise is real: a payment the bank cannot recall still promises an undo. A student can predict the finding in words from Plate 10.3 and Rule 79. The two lawful repairs end on a question with no single answer ("Which of the two is true of your bank"). That is a good prompt for a seminar. The student cannot predict the code name IRREVERSIBLE_CLAIMS_UNDO from this page, but the instruction asks for an expectation, not a code, so the exercise holds.
5. **Verdict.** `ready after the items above`. The teaching line is clear, but the rule count and an undefined term stop a careful student. The sentence that stopped me most: "Rule 129, The Action Is the Last Thing to Go" against "Zayn's constitution of 123 rules".

## Chapter 11, The strata of expression

1. **What I remember.** Between the intent and the drawing sit six functions: structure, signal, role, motion, message, tempo. Each names a meaning, never an appearance, and each must serve an intent or it is refused (EXPRESSION_ORPHAN). Today two are fields of their own (SIGNALS, ROLE), three are carried by older fields, and tempo lives in the law. I could restate that split and the table without looking.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "Signal: a meaning, never a colour": "five statuses", and the page names only danger and warning. The other three are not on the page.
   - "Message, structure and tempo": TONE has "a closed list", and the list is not given. The exercise needs it (see 4).
   - "Tempo stands in the law": "Rules 81 and 91 hold the wait and business time" goes past in a clause, with nothing for a student to hold.
   - Rule 125, against the "123 rules" of the door paragraph (as in chapter 10).
3. **Where I doubted.**
   - "Its contract decides three kinds of finding from SIGNALS and seven from ROLE": the page shows two of the seven by name. The claim is larger than what the page shows.
   - The court lists ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN, "A reversible control keeps no reachable UNDO". Every other refused fixture has its "twin"; this one has none, and the page does not say why.
4. **What was mine.** Is the exercise real, and does the student have what it asks? The purge contains three findings. A student can predict the widget word (Plates 11.3 and 11.4) and the motion that reports nothing (Plates 11.7 and 11.8). The third, "a signal's word where a tone goes", needs the tone list. The page never gives it, and the right word, critical, first appears in the answer. A student who read only this page would guess. The closing question ("Which is it?") and the remark that "a shake is the right way" is a person's judgment both work well in class.
5. **Verdict.** `ready after the items above`. The order is the clearest in the part, but the exercise asks for a list that the page withholds. The sentence that stopped me most: "a signal's word where a tone goes".

## For the part

- **Hand-over.** Chapter 10 closes on The Second Founding and "the next chapter takes them one by one". Chapter 11 opens on the same proposal. Chapter 11 closes on Part V, "carried in 10 channels". Both hand-overs work. Chapter 11's "a fourth finding may appear" picks up chapter 10's exercise without naming its code. That is a good echo for a student who did both exercises.
- **Contradiction.** Plate 10.3 is `ROLE reversible` with `ALWAYS_REACHABLE [CANCEL]`, and the verifier answers only "MISSING_UNDO". Chapter 11's court says that the same shape gets ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN: "A reversible control keeps no reachable UNDO." A student who reads both asks why Plate 10.3 gets one finding and not two. Neither page says when each code applies.
- **Contradiction, the count.** Both door paragraphs say "123 rules". Chapter 10 cites Rule 129 and chapter 11 cites Rule 125. The page needs one clause on how the rules are numbered.
- **Clearest plate.** Plate 10.2: the same file as 10.1, clean, under a drawing that carries seven named faults. A student sees the line at once.
- **Hardest plate.** Plate 11.9, the purge: three findings at once, one of which needs the TONE list that the page does not give.
- **What the educator takes away.** I could teach these two chapters as one unit in a single session: what a declaration can state, what only a drawing shows, and the six strata as the vocabulary between them, with the "Stage" table as the summary on the board. What a student needs from earlier: chapters 4, 7 and 8 (levels, TONE and SCREEN, the irreversible role). Both "In your hands" sections end on a question that the verifier cannot settle, and that is the best material in the part for class discussion. Two `[AUTHOR: …]` lines in each chapter are visible to the student. In each chapter one of them asks whether the exercise is final. I would wait for that answer before setting the exercise as homework.

# Part IV, second reading: the AI agent that reads the book as data

Read 2026-11-27: chapter 10, then chapter 11, as built text (`.cache/as-read/`). Read once whole, then again against the brief.

## Chapter 10, What a declaration cannot see

1. **What I remember.** A declaration states intent and names no widget, so one clean file can sit under a careful drawing and under one with seven faults; Plates 10.1 and 10.2 are the same file and both run clean, at level 2 and at level 4. A rule splits in two: the existence of an undo is refused in the declaration (MISSING_UNDO), and where the undo sits is measured on the painted page or judged by a person.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "One defect gets one finding" (One rule, two halves). The page scopes three codes apart. But the contract box at the court prints ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN as "A reversible control keeps no reachable UNDO." Plate 10.3 fits that sentence too (reversible, no UNDO), yet it gets only MISSING_UNDO. If I write from the contract's words alone, I predict two findings on Plate 10.3. The scope ("declares no act") is stated only in the prose.
   - "a control that acts may not promise to change nothing" (Rule 119). This page never says which ROLE values promise to change nothing. Chapter 11 lists five, and `navigate` "never what is true" could fall either side. I cannot tell whether `navigate` plus ACT gets ROLE_ACTS_WITHOUT_CONSEQUENCE.
   - The exemption I need most is shown but never stated: an act with ROLE irreversible and no UNDO is clean (Plate 10.7). The list of three cases does not include it.
   - "level 2 needs the file alone, level 3 a table..., level 4 a painted page": level 1 is not defined here, and chapter 11 prints level-1 codes reported in a level-2 run. That levels are cumulative is implied but never said.
3. **Where I doubted.** Nothing reads as an advertisement. "Five of the seven faults meet a check" holds when I pair them myself: 119/K16, red/K09 and K10, 105/K01, 123/K03, 40/K19. K02's contrast matches none of the seven faults, so it is listed as extra.
4. **What was mine.** The boundary between what a declaration states and what the drawing shows, as a rule I can apply. Mostly I got it. The code table and Plates 10.3 to 10.8 give me MISSING_UNDO and IRREVERSIBLE_CLAIMS_UNDO exactly. ROLE_ACTS_WITHOUT_CONSEQUENCE and ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN stay underdetermined.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The contract wording of ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN is wider than the behaviour the page shows. Sentence that stopped me most: "The contract scopes the three apart."

## Chapter 11, The strata of expression

1. **What I remember.** Six strata are functions between the intent and the medium. SIGNALS and ROLE are the verifier's expression fields, and each has a refusal for a word outside its closed list (UNKNOWN_SIGNAL, UNKNOWN_ROLE) and the binding rule EXPRESSION_ORPHAN. Motion, message and structure are carried by older fields with their own checks, and tempo stays in the law (Rules 18, 125).
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "Every expression declaration traces to an intent, or it is an error" (The principle). Plate 11.11's FEEDBACK has TONE and no SATISFIES, and it runs clean. So do the FEEDBACKs in 11.7 and 11.8, which have MOTION and no intent. The rule binds only SIGNALS and ROLE, but that limit is said ten plates later: "The verifier holds two expression fields, SIGNALS and ROLE". I would put that scope next to the principle.
   - "three kinds of finding from SIGNALS and seven from ROLE": I cannot list them from the page. For ROLE I find six (UNKNOWN_ROLE, ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN, IRREVERSIBLE_CLAIMS_UNDO, ROLE_ACTS_WITHOUT_CONSEQUENCE, ROLE_UNDECLARED, EXPRESSION_ORPHAN). For SIGNALS I find two.
   - Line numbers follow no rule the page states. UNKNOWN_ROLE and UNKNOWN_SIGNAL point at the field's line (purge.zayn:4, invoices.zayn:3). INVALID_TONE points at the declaration's line (purge.zayn:7, where TONE is line 8). A checker that has to match reports needs the convention.
   - INVALID_TONE cites no rule or article, while every other refusal on the two pages cites one. A reason in one clause would settle it.
3. **Where I doubted.** "Two are the proposal's fields" (opening) against "two of the six are the language's own fields today" (closing). Same fact, two owners: I would keep one word.
4. **What was mine.** The closed lists, stated once: Rule 118's five statuses with their meanings, the five roles with their promises, the five tones. I got all three exactly, and the table under "Where it stands" gives each stratum's field and stage. I could write a SIGNALS, ROLE or TONE value from memory.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The principle is stated wider than the fields it binds. Sentence that stopped me most: "Every expression declaration traces to an intent, or it is an error."

## For the part

**Hand-over.** Chapter 10 ends on The Second Founding and "the next chapter takes them one by one", and chapter 11 opens by defining a stratum. Chapter 11 closes on Part V's ten channels. Both hand over.

**Contradiction.** Chapter 10's Where it stands lists under *designed*: "the strata of expression between the intent and the drawing, the subject of chapter 11". Chapter 11's table puts five strata at "built". Chapter 10 itself runs SIGNALS as a built field. Chapter 10's line should say what is designed: "the rest of each stratum as the proposal states it".

**Agreement checked.** The 22 verbs, the 123 rules numbered to 130, Rule 82, Plates 10.7 and 11.10 (an irreversible act with no UNDO is clean), the audit's 37 = 32 + 5 (the table sums correctly), and MENU_LANDING_NOT_FIRST under Rule 116 all agree.

**Clearest plate.** 11.1 to 11.2: one wrong word ("red"), one refusal quoting Rule 118, one repair by meaning. I could generate the lawful form without reading on.

**Hardest plate.** 10.5. It is clean, and the defect it carries lives only in the drawing. For an agent that checks declarations, it teaches what not to refuse. That is right, but it is the hardest to turn into a rule.

**What this reader takes away.** A procedure:
- Refuse colours in SIGNALS and widgets in ROLE.
- Require an intent beside SIGNALS or ROLE.
- Require UNDO with ACT unless the role is irreversible, and refuse UNDO when it is.
- Require REPORTS with MOTION.
- Leave placement, size, colour and wording to the drawing.

Three rules still need one sentence each: which roles "change nothing", the scope of ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN in the contract's own words, and what level 1 reads.

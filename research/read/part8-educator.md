# Part VIII, The Commons: read from the educator's door

Reader: the educator (teaches interface design or programming). Pages read in order, twice: chapter 20, then chapter 21, from
`.cache/as-read/`. Read on 2026-11-27.

## Chapter 20, From a remark to a rule

**What I remember.** A remark becomes evidence when it is kept with its words, date, place and cause; the corpus of 489 records
holds such remarks, and three followed here led to a refusal (Rule 112), an amended rule a person reads (Rule 106), and a
proposal that waits (the 18 px floor). The proof is the glide refused and the jump clean, plates 20.1 and 20.2.

**Where I lost the thread.**
- "What a record keeps": five numbers in two paragraphs (235, 489, 174, 217, 26). Then "The law holds 26 rules numbered above
  104" under the next heading: the same 26 for a different thing. A student mixes them.
- "Two records and a cause": "Rule 106 is filed under machine-later, the tier of a rule whose instrument is to come". "Tier" here
  is a tier of enforcement; chapter 21 opens with "tier" as core, commons, local.
- "The capture, designed": "an operator meets a pain, in the app, at the moment it hurtscapture: the surface" reads run together
  (a diagram lost in the text?).
- Terms used before they are said, for a reader who opens here: "operator" (Article VII, and the capture), "the painted page"
  ("met on the painted page, by a person"), "lint field", "ROLE reversible", "SATISFIES [SELECT]", "ALWAYS_REACHABLE [UNDO]",
  "TONE" (first seen in the exercise, plate 20.4). The door paragraph covers declaration, law, verifier, finding, record only.

**Where I doubted.**
- The opening promises "where three records led"; the heading is "Two records and a cause"; then "Each of the three". Which?
- "the menu remark of 8 November had its checks the same day" against, under "The records behind the rules", "Rule 116 gathered
  five records over three months before its checks were written". Both may be true; side by side they read as a contradiction.
- "Two lawful repairs follow" introduces plate 20.6, a glide that Rule 112 lists among what it avoids ("Fades, slides, and
  reveals when content arrives"). Clean is not lawful; the page itself says "only the first is what the record asked".
- "The field records the error and the change to the text": the postmortem quoted above records "the root cause(s)" too.

**What was mine.** Is the exercise real? Yes, for its first half: plate 20.4 is a predict-then-check task a student can do, and
20.6 teaches the best lesson of the part, that the verifier checks the declaration and a person checks the glide. The closing
task, "write the record yourself, in the corpus's fields", is a good classroom exercise; its answer to "Would you mark it a
repeat?" is on the page (the 1 October record "carry repeat false") but the exercise does not point back to it.

**Verdict.** ready after the items above. The order is followable and the exercise is real; the vocabulary and two numeric
clashes trip a student. Sentence that stopped me most: "Rule 106 is filed under machine-later, the tier of a rule whose
instrument is to come."

## Chapter 21, Core, commons, local

**What I remember.** The proposal designs four tiers (statute, core, commons, local) under one rule: a local law may be stricter
than the commons and never looser. One case is built today: a VIBE that hides a verb another declaration keeps within reach is
refused (plates 21.1 and 21.2); the pin and the team's contract are built, and the contract can loosen the verifier. I could
restate the four tiers and the supremacy rule without looking.

**Where I lost the thread.**
- "Four tiers": the commons is "today's rules of the law and every rule a team adds"; the local is a team's "own laws", and "What I
  refused" says "A team's rule enters the commons or stays local." Where does a team's added rule sit?
- "A place may add, not take away": "may hide verbs". "Verb" is not said for a drop-in reader; "families of verbs" in the core
  list is not either.
- "A contract of its own": "lowered the culture's refusal above to a warning" (which refusal: VIBE_STRIPS_RIGHT, I infer).

**Where I doubted.**
- "In your hands": "If your team needed a channel the list does not hold, which tier would its request enter". The page says a new
  medium is "a change of the language or an amendment of the law", but never says whether the closed list of media belongs to the
  core or the commons. A student cannot answer; I cannot grade it.
- The exercise's second finding, UNKNOWN_MEDIUM, needs the list of ten media, which the page does not print or point to. The
  setup ("named its own medium") is the hint; a pointer to where the ten are listed would make it fair.
- "The verifier follows the contract a team gives it" with the looser contract passing: stated plainly, good. But the runs are
  research records, not something a class can reproduce from the page.

**What was mine.** Could a student follow this order? Yes: field, principle, the four tiers, admission, one built check, what a
team holds. Plates 21.1/21.2 are the clearest pair in the part: one word changed (UNDO to ZOOM), refusal to clean. Plate 21.3 is a
real exercise with two findings and the count given ("Two findings wait").

**Verdict.** ready after the items above. Sentence that stopped me most: "which tier would its request enter: your local law,
the commons with its three forms, or the core?"

## The part

- **Hand-over.** Chapter 20 ends "The next chapter takes the tiers a rule passes through"; chapter 21 picks it up and points back
  ("chapter 20 tells its first step, the capture of a remark"). Chapter 21 hands to Part IX cleanly.
- **Contradictions.** "Tier": ch20 "machine-later, the tier of a rule whose instrument is to come" vs ch21 "A tier of the law is
  the level at which a rule is written and kept". Within ch20: "checks the same day" vs "five records over three months before
  its checks were written".
- **Clearest plate:** 21.1/21.2. **Hardest:** 20.3, clean, its amended rule not checkable, and ROLE reversible unexplained; a
  student sees a clean report and learns nothing it tested.
- **What my reader takes away.** A teachable loop (remark, record, rule, check) and a teachable boundary (a place may add, never
  take away), each with one runnable pair. Before I set either as homework I need the terms said at the door and every closing
  question answerable from the page.

## The three items that most need fixing

1. "Tier" carries two meanings across the part (machine-later in ch20, core/commons/local in ch21): name ch20's sense otherwise.
2. Closing questions a student cannot answer from the page: ch21's "which tier", with the list of media unplaced; ch20's
   "Two lawful repairs" calls a glide lawful that Rule 112 avoids.
3. Terms before they are said for a drop-in reader: operator, painted page, lint field, ROLE, TONE, SATISFIES, ALWAYS_REACHABLE,
   verb; and ch20's "three records" vs "Two records and a cause".

# Part IV, second reading: the designer who does not program

Read: chapter 10, *What a declaration cannot see*; chapter 11, *The strata of expression*. Read whole twice, as built (`.cache/as-read/`).

## Chapter 10: What a declaration cannot see

1. **What I remember.** One clean declaration can sit under a careful drawing and under one with seven faults, and the verifier passes both, because it reads what the user does and not how it is drawn. A rule like 79 splits: the verifier refuses a missing undo, and the audit of the painted page or a person judges where the undo sits.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - Opening: "carrying the seven faults the record names". "The record" is never said; it comes back as "the record's word" and "The record shows".
   - One rule, two halves: "The contract scopes the three apart." I learn what the contract is only in chapter 11.
   - One rule, two halves: the three codes in one paragraph (MISSING_UNDO, ROLE_PROMISE_BROKEN, IRREVERSIBLE_CLAIMS_UNDO). The contract's own line for the second, "A reversible control keeps no reachable UNDO", fits plate 10.3 exactly too, so I had to reread to see why 10.3 gets the first.
   - One declaration, two drawings: "Chapter 4 explains the run; here it shows the line." I could not tell what "it" is.
   - What reads the drawing: "in both of the site's cultures, the two ways it draws the same interface". I guess two languages, but the page does not say so.
   - Where the field leads: "on a same-origin web page, after the fact". "Same-origin" means nothing to me.
   - Where it stands: "rules moved up the enforcement ladder". It points forward and I don't need it now, so this is not a fault.
3. **Where I doubted.** "Zayn leads in the division... Zayn binds both to one law". Two paragraphs earlier the page says the audit reads "the pages of one site, Zayn's own". The claim holds at its size, but I needed both sentences together. "Where it stands" gives the Built items twice, once as lines and once as prose.
4. **What was mine.** The two drawings. Plate 10.2's sketch tags each fault with its rule (Approve, Export and Schedule against Rule 8; Undo inside "More" against Rule 79), and I could explain to a colleague why the file stays clean. The pairing of faults with K-checks shows me which of my review items a machine takes and which two stay mine. Got it.
5. **Verdict.** `ready after the items above`. The point lands. "The record" and "the contract" are used before they are said, and the three-code paragraph needs a second pass. The sentence that stopped me most: "The contract scopes the three apart."

## Chapter 11: The strata of expression

1. **What I remember.** Between what the user does and what a medium draws there are six functions: structure, signal, role, motion, message and tempo. Each names a meaning, never a look. Red is refused and danger accepted, button is refused and reversible accepted, and a slide that reports no state is refused.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - Six functions in the middle: "the founding architecture of the platform ran from the intent". Which platform? It is not named on this page.
   - Tempo: "from a measurement of the sound plane". I don't know what the sound plane is.
   - Tempo: "Rules 81 and 91 hold the wait and business time". I don't know what "business time" is.
   - What I refused: "the proposal's amendment to the reference design, so that the law's layer holds the vocabulary". I don't know "reference design" or "the law's layer".
   - The court: UNKNOWN_SIGNAL and UNKNOWN_ROLE are "level 1". Chapter 10 says what levels 2, 3 and 4 need, but never level 1.
   - Message: "chapter 7 runs that refusal and shows why TONE and SIGNALS keep two lists". The exercise turns on exactly that confusion (TONE danger), and the why is not given here.
3. **Where I doubted.** "Its contract decides three kinds of finding from SIGNALS and seven from ROLE". The page shows one and three, so the count is larger than what I can see. The heading "What I refused" opens with "I ruled parts of the proposal into the law", which is not a refusal. "Where it stands" says the Built items three times: as lines, as a table and as prose.
4. **What was mine.** Signal as meaning, never colour. Plates 11.1 and 11.2 are what my design system's status tokens should have been. The five statuses, each in the rule's own words ("watch this"), and "Expression is not appearance" are what I take into a review. Got it.
5. **Verdict.** `ready after the items above`. The six strata and their stages could be restated without looking. Four phrases in "Tempo" and "What I refused" are opaque. The sentence that stopped me most: "from a measurement of the sound plane".

## The part

**Hand-over.** Chapter 10 ends on "the next chapter takes them one by one", and chapter 11 opens on the strata. Chapter 11 ends on "Part V turns to the media", which matches "Chapter 12 carries a signal across the media". Both hand-overs hold.

**Contradiction.** Chapter 10's "Where it stands" puts the strata under Designed: "the strata of expression between the intent and the drawing, the subject of chapter 11". Chapter 11 says "two of the six are the language's own fields today, three are carried by other fields", and its table marks five as built. A reader of chapter 10 alone thinks the whole middle layer is unbuilt. Chapter 10 needs to say "the strata as The Second Founding states them", as chapter 11 does.

**Smaller tension.** Chapter 10 uses ROLE in plates 10.3 to 10.8, but the five roles are listed only in chapter 11. Chapter 10's gloss ("reversible promises that what it changes can be taken back") is enough for its plates, so this is not a fault.

**Clearest plate.** 11.1 and 11.2, overdue invoices: red refused, danger and warning accepted, and the "In words" line says what the red was for.

**Hardest plate.** 10.3, the approve control with no way back. The finding is clear, but the paragraph after it brings in two more codes and a contract I have not met.

**What I take away.** A clean declaration is not a well-drawn screen. The verifier holds the intent, its meanings and its promises. The audit measures part of the drawing (32 checks at six widths). The rest is my review: the competing main buttons, where the undo sits, and whether a shake suits a purge. In my next design review I would name status by meaning and controls by their promise, and keep the drawing's questions for people.

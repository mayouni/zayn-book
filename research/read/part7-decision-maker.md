# Part VII, The Law: read from the door of the decision-maker who commissions software

Read 2026-11-23, chapters 16 to 19 as built, twice each. My question: what can I require of a supplier, what is checked and when, what adoption costs.

## Chapter 16, The constitution, part by part

1. **Remembered.** The law is one data file, and every count and citation is read from it. Proof: four refusals whose citations I followed into the file (MISSING_UNDO, Rule 79, Article IV, the right to reverse).
2. **Lost.** "One rule, field by field": "it says amended where the lineage says canonized". Canonized is said only in chapter 18. Every report ends "at level 2", and no page of the part says what a level is. A level is what I would write into a contract. Set 10, "stzErrors Philosophy", is printed with no word on what it means.
3. **Doubted.** The principle says "the refusal that cites it all read it there". The exercise shows VERB_NOT_CARRIED citing Article II for a rule the file puts under Article III, because the contract supplies the article. The principle claims more than the page shows.
4. **Mine.** What I can demand: stable numbers ("a citation of Rule 79 names the same rule in every version"), exit codes 1 and 0, and a machine form with `cites`. Got it.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** Stopped me most: "it says amended where the lineage says canonized".

## Chapter 17, The enforcement ladder

1. **Remembered.** Each rule is filed under one of four tiers. By citation, the verifier answers 13 rules and the audits 8, 20 in all, and a person can judge every rule. Proof: Plate 17.11, an assistant that accepts its own refund passes clean, and "a person sees it".
2. **Lost.** "its value is one of four words": only judgment is defined; machine, tool and machine-later never are, in the chapter that reads them. The table prints "K22 LAYOUT_STILL", the text LAYOUT_DISPLACEMENT. "The design system's record of its second milestone": which design system?
3. **Doubted.** "A declaration states no colours, so the verifier could never read it" keeps Rule 62 out of machine, yet "So Rule 38, filed under machine, is answered by the audit." If machine includes the audit, Rule 62's reason falls. And "20 rules in all" is never set against 123: say that the other 103 are held by a person.
4. **Mine.** What is checked, and when: two moments, three instruments. Got it. My most useful line is buried in "Where the field leads": two surfaces "differing by seven broken laws" were one declaration, both clean. It belongs with the instruments. Whether the audit runs on my supplier's product is not said.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The tier words, the chapter's subject, stay undefined. Stopped me most: "So Rule 38, filed under machine, is answered by the audit."

## Chapter 18, Amendment from evidence

1. **Remembered.** The law changes only on evidence. Each change is a dated entry, evidence is never corrected, and numbers are never reused. Proof: the conflict between Rule 3 and Rule 118, run as UNKNOWN_SIGNAL.
2. **Lost.** The part names four versions: release v0.2.0, law 3.17, contract 1.11 (Plate 16.7) and "language 1.4". None is set beside the others, so I do not know which to pin. "Rule 95, a rule of the field culture" collides with the book's "the field".
3. **Doubted.** Semantic Versioning is introduced ("moves the first number when a change breaks") and Zayn's practice is never stated. Version 3.16 added three refusals in a minor step, so a build clean at 3.15 can fail at 3.16. My contract needs that said. Plate 18.9: deleting one line turns a refusal clean, so a supplier can pass by declaring less; the lesson is not drawn.
4. **Mine.** How to pin, and who rules: "The version names; the hash proves." and "On Zayn's docket I rule alone". Got both.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** Stopped me most: the Semantic Versioning sentence, left without Zayn's answer.

## Chapter 19, How to write a rule

1. **Remembered.** Seven steps turn a remark into a rule, ending in a template. Proof: the menu remark, followed to Rule 116's checks, with a refusal on each bound (19.1, 19.4) and clean between them (19.5).
2. **Lost.** Nothing that stopped me. "Chapter 13 gives the grid" comes with enough of a clause.
3. **Doubted.** "the house that publishes this book adopted the same three kinds of finding": the part's only adoption, by the author's own house, plainly stated. "a check proves itself on the day it fails on the defect" stands beside a last plate where "Stock now", the shape of "Try here", passes; the page owns it.
4. **Mine.** Can my team write its own rules? Yes: the template is "written for a team that keeps its rules in a document of its own". What the seven steps cost is not said.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** Stopped me most: "the check that my second remark produced passes a menu of the shape that my first remark gave".

## The part

**Handovers.** All hold. Chapter 16 ends "The next chapter reads them", chapter 17 ends on promotion as a change of the law, and chapter 18 ends "follows one, field by field".

**Contradictions.** (a) The tier "machine", in chapter 17, quoted above. (b) Chapter 18: "On 8 November 2026 I looked at a menu ... and wrote a remark", yet its "the menu I had refused in my remark" is the repeated label, which chapter 19 dates "of 6 November 2026". (c) Chapter 16's principle against its own exercise.

**Clearest plate:** 17.11, where one clean report shows what is left to a person. **Hardest:** 16.9, four identical findings that cite Rule 82 under Article II, ending in an open question.

**Takeaway.** I can require declarations that pass the verifier against a law pinned by version and hash, with stable citations and a machine-readable report. Instruments answer 20 of 123 rules today; a named reviewer holds the rest, the right to a human among them. Not taken away: what level to require, which version to pin, whether a minor version breaks a build, what binds a declaration to the delivered interface, what adoption costs. In every page the `[AUTHOR: …]` stake sits where a buyer looks for the reason to adopt.

# Part VI, The Readers: read from the designer's door

Reader: the designer who does not program. Pages read twice, in order: chapter 14, then chapter 15.

## Chapter 14, Two cultures, one law

**What I remember.** A culture (the law's "vibe") may change how a screen looks, its density, colours and type, but it may not hide a way back that a flow keeps within reach. The proof is Plate 14.2: Retro hides the undo and the verifier refuses the whole file, even though Modern alone is lawful.

**Where I lost the thread.**
- "A culture, declared": "And it may hide verbs with SUPPRESSES, 'Legal only for intents no declaration guarantees'". The page says verbs, the quote says intents, the finding says "suppresses an intent". I could not tell if a verb and an intent are the same thing.
- "Two cultures, one site": "The law's notes name them curator and guardian." Which is which, and what it means for me, is never said.
- "Two cultures, one site": "The six operator rights" is quoted; "operator" is never said in a clause, and Article VI's title uses it too.
- Every report: "at level 2". Levels are not said on this page.
- "What a culture may not carry": "TONE critical". What a tone is, and what "critical" is among its values, is not said.
- "Where the field leads": "the request to read every file of a run is with the Zayn session". This is a real limit for me (a culture in its own file passes), but "the Zayn session" is a word from the workshop, not from the book.
- "In your hands": the exercise asks me to predict three findings, and one of them, DECORATIVE_ANIMATION, appears nowhere before it. "MOTION none" is shown, but no sentence says motion must report a state. I could not have written that finding.

**Where I doubted.**
- "In your hands", after Plate 14.6: "Rule 112, on motion, is a person's to judge." Plate 14.4 has just printed a machine refusal citing Rule 112. Both cannot hold for me without a sentence that says what part the machine checks and what part the person judges.
- "Where it stands": the Built, Designed, Next list is printed, then printed again almost word for word as a paragraph. I read the same 120 words twice.

**What was mine.** Whether a theme can take something away from my users, and who stops it. I got it: "the vibe owns how the screen looks, and it has no freedom over what the screen shows." I could explain the Plate 14.2 refusal to a design review without the book open. The author's remark on the browser's blue selected line in Retro is exactly a design review finding, and I recognised it.

**Verdict.** Ready after the items above: the principle and its proof land; the exercise asks for a finding the page never taught, and Rule 112 reads as both machine and person. Sentence that stopped me most: "Rule 112, on motion, is a person's to judge."

## Chapter 15, Culture as an axis

**What I remember.** A declaration names what a mark means (success, warning, danger, info, muted), never its colour, so "green" and "red" are refused and the colour is left to each culture's rendering. A flag used to label a language is forbidden by the law, but the verifier passes it (Plate 15.3): a person refuses it.

**Where I lost the thread.**
- "Three questions about a sign": "What does it do: that is its stratum, chapter 11." Stratum is never said in a clause here, and "A status of danger is a signal, the stratum" made it harder, not easier.
- "A meaning, never a reading": "SIGNALS names the meanings its records carry". Records of what? A screen's rows, I guessed.
- "What the law holds": "The law files Rule 56 under machine-later". The tier is said in the same sentence, good; "a meaning-to-rendering path" and "derivation path" are not.
- "In your hands", the closing: "in Rule 118's second family, the quiet one". The first family is the five statuses; the second is never named on the page.
- "In your hands": again a finding I was never shown, EXPRESSION_ORPHAN, "An expression field is declared on a declaration that satisfies no intent." Nothing earlier says a screen with SIGNALS must also say what the user does.
- "The court": UNKNOWN_SIGNAL is "level 1", but the report above it says "at level 2".

**Where I doubted.**
- "A meaning, never a reading": the language is "already culture-safe on that axis by construction". The same page shows a flag menu that runs clean and is forbidden. The claim is true for colour words only; the sentence after it says so, but the quote reads larger.
- "Who decides": "The verifier decides one thing on this axis: the colour word". The court beside it lists a second machine refusal, SIGNAL_UNPERCEIVABLE.

**What was mine.** Where cultural meaning lives when I design for more than one audience. I got it, and it is the best moment of the part for me: Plate 15.3 with "Flag of Egypt" for Arabic, then "Français" and "العربية" as the lawful labels. The variance and hazard split (red as danger or prosperity; a sovereign sign never a label) I could teach to my team.

**Verdict.** Ready after the items above: the meaning-versus-colour point is the clearest of the part; stratum and the second family are used unsaid, and the exercise again asks for an untaught finding. Sentence that stopped me most: "A status of danger is a signal, the stratum; it is carried in colour".

## For the part

**Hand-over.** Chapter 14 ends on "the culture the reader brings", and chapter 15 opens on exactly that, naming the two senses of the word. Chapter 14's last section on Set 18 points to 15, and 15 picks up Rules 63, 64 and 122. The hand-over holds.

**Contradictions.**
- Chapter 14: "the vibe owns how the screen looks" and Rule 112 refused by the verifier, against "Rule 112, on motion, is a person's to judge." (same page, see above).
- Chapter 14, Rule 64 keeps "the controls of a player" from turning; chapter 15 quotes "media transport controls" and adds "any progress that encodes a physical direction". Not a contradiction, but chapter 14's list is shorter; a designer copying it would miss progress bars.
- Chapter 15, "The verifier decides one thing on this axis", against its own court listing SIGNAL_UNPERCEIVABLE.

**Clearest plate.** Plates 15.1 and 15.2: green and red refused, success and danger accepted, and the specification's line "Which colour, timbre or waveform serves danger is the rendering's business". I could restate it without looking.

**Hardest plate.** Plate 14.4: the blink is refused under a rule the page then says a person judges, and the finding was never shown before.

**What I take away.** A culture is a costume over the same rights: it may change every pixel but not take away undo or cancel. A declaration says what a mark means, the culture says how it looks, and some signs, a flag for a language, are a person's to refuse because the machine reads only words.

## The three items that most need fixing

1. Both exercises expect a finding the page never introduced: DECORATIVE_ANIMATION (Plate 14.4) and EXPRESSION_ORPHAN (Plate 15.4). Say before each exercise, in a sentence, that motion must report a state and that a mark needs a stated user action.
2. Rule 112: the verifier refuses under it, then the page says "a person's to judge". Say which part the machine checks.
3. Terms used unsaid: stratum, Rule 118's second family, operator, curator and guardian, verb against intent, level 2, "the Zayn session".

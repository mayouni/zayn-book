# Part II, The Heritage: read from the educator's door

Read twice, in order: chapters 3, 4, 5. Question held: could a student follow this order, what does each page assume, which exercise is real.

## Chapter 3, What the field wrote

1. **What I remember.** Nielsen, Shneiderman, the two gulfs and Tufte map onto numbered rules. Where a declaration can state the rule, the verifier refuses by name: invisible state, no undo, a selection that pays, a status outside the five. The size of a target, grouping and ink are left to the painted page.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "SATISFIES [LOCATE]" (State the user must see): the verbs are first called "the law's 22 verbs" at the map, after six plates.
   - "the right to know" (plate 3.1 report): a right is used in the reports and never said.
   - "Article III" (State the user must see): what an article is, beside a rule, is never said.
   - "Both run clean at level 4" (Where the field leads): only levels 2 and 3 were defined. The court lists UNKNOWN_SIGNAL at "level 1", which is not defined either.
   - "Rule 51 judgment (Article II)" (the map): the parenthesis is unexplained.
3. **Where I doubted.** The tier definition: a tier names "the instrument that owes its check". But "The verifier checks Rules 82, 79, 26, 51 and 16 by name", and the map files 51 and 16 under judgment. The page explains Rule 16 through Article II, and Rule 118 through "judges a word of the list". A student will still ask what "judgment" means if a machine refuses. Also "What Zayn adds, in the site's words: 'Refusal.'" reads as a slogan.
4. **What was mine.** A real exercise. Plate 3.12 needs exactly the three refusals the chapter ran, at the level and with the table already given. A student can write the three codes before opening the answer. Got it. The closing question ("can a payment be undone?") is a good discussion prompt, though its answer is in chapter 8.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** Stopped most by: "The verifier checks Rules 82, 79, 26, 51 and 16 by name".

## Chapter 4, The instruments of the field

1. **What I remember.** Each instrument of the field reads one object: the criterion reads the target, the token its value, the story the rendered state, the linter the markup, the visual test pixels, CLS motion. A declaration is another object, intent. Two plates (a selection that pays, an inert card that acts) are refused even though every instrument could pass their drawing, and a clean run at level 4 still leaves the painted page to the audit.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "The field is lint, and 18 rules carry it. Its heads name the instrument" (The law names its instruments): "field" here is a data field. For three chapters "the field" has meant the discipline. "Heads" is never said. I read the paragraph three times.
   - "Softanza's graphics plane", "a manifest lint, owed to read the profile a project's manifest names": both unexplained.
   - "the impact fraction of D multiplied by the distance fraction of D": D is never said, so the quote does not teach.
   - "level 4 ... is 'Projection', and it requires 'a rendered surface'", then a level-4 run in which "every one of them reads the declaration": I could not tell a student what level 4 is.
   - "K22 LAYOUT_STILL", "K17 TARGET": these codes come with no gloss.
   - "ACTOR operator" (plate 4.1): chapter 3 taught that operator is the verifier's word for the user. Here it is an actor's name.
   - The Rule 112 sentence under plate 4.3 is hard to parse.
3. **Where I doubted.** In "What I refused", the first item ("I moved a menu's structure") is not a refusal.
4. **What was mine.** Plate 4.6 is a real exercise: three findings, two lawful repairs that say different things, and a closing question a class can argue over. One gap: ROLE_ACTS_WITHOUT_CONSEQUENCE was shown only for inert. A student must infer that navigate also "promises to change nothing", and only the tangled Rule 112 sentence says so. Mostly got it.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** Stopped most by: "The field is lint, and 18 rules carry it."

## Chapter 5, The road sign

1. **What I remember.** The Vienna Convention closes its vocabulary, explains its form by the stranger's understanding, and amends on memorandum and experiment. Zayn's closed list of media gives signage three verbs, so the verifier refuses an act, a way back or READ on a sign, a medium off the list and a sixth meaning.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article II" (plate 5.1): why does a sign that cannot open a gate break Perceivable State? Nothing says.
   - "natively, at a cost, or not at all" (A sign in Zayn's data): "at a cost" is never shown.
   - "Distinguished from print by election" (the quoted note): "election" is a hard word for a non-native student. It is listed later as a property but never said.
3. **Where I doubted.** Nothing inflated. The handling of the outline's "sixty years" line is exemplary. The treaty is read "at this size", clause by clause.
4. **What was mine.** The pair 5.3 and 5.4 (one verb, READ, turns a sign into print) is the clearest lesson in the part. The exercise 5.9 repeats plate 5.1 (EXIT refused on signage). A student who read 5.1 knows the answer. Its value is the closing distinction ("the word on the sign and the verb of the law are two things"), and that could be the question asked.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** Stopped most by: "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article II".

## For the part

- **Hand-overs.** Each page hands over. Chapter 3 ends on "it also built instruments", which opens chapter 4. Chapter 4 ends on the sign "read at speed by strangers". Chapter 5 hands over to the inversion.
- **Contradictions.**
  - Rule 82's article. Chapter 3: "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article III · the right to know". Chapter 5: "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article II".
  - Target size. Chapter 3: "A person judges each of these rules on the painted page", with Rule 19 machine-later. Chapter 4: "Its check K17 TARGET sets a size for a target", which is a built audit.
  - "Operator". Chapter 3: "the language's word for the person this book calls the user". Chapter 4: "ACTOR operator".
- **Clearest plate:** 5.3 and 5.4. **Hardest:** 4.3, a report citing three rules and an article, one of them on motion.
- **Order for a class.** It works: advice, then instruments, then a law that holds. Each chapter teaches by an offending plate and its lawful twin, which is exactly the shape of a lab. Each chapter repeats the four-word door paragraph, so a single chapter can be assigned alone. What a student takes away: the field's advice and Zayn's rules hold the same knowledge. A rule refuses only where a declaration can state it, and the drawing keeps its own instruments. What the student cannot yet do is say what a tier, an article, a right or a level means. The part uses all four as working words.

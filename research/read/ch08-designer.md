# Chapter 8, read by a designer who does not program

Read twice, 2026-11-21. Nothing changed, nothing run.

## 1. What I remember

A refusal is a checking program's answer to a few lines stating what a user does on a surface. It gives a code and, where the law has one, a rule, an article and a user's right, so the answer is to repair the lines or amend the law. The proof: five declarations run, their reports printed, among them a payment approval refused twice and clean after one added line. It stops at the lines: the drawn screen belongs to other tools.

## 2. Where I lost the thread

- Stamp above the first paragraph: "5 of 5 declarations kept their promise". Three of the five were refused. There, and in "the verifier answered what this declaration promises", a promise is the page's expected answer; on Plate 8.4 ("promises an undo") it is the user's due.
- *The refusal*: "the verifier's answer to a declaration that breaks the law". Only the declaration is defined; the verifier, the law and who wrote it are not. Versions collide: "Zayn v0.2.0", "law 3.17", "against Zayn v3.17".
- *The field*: "A linter holds named rules and a severity for each." Linter, ESLint, exit code, build and JSX arrive together; the exit code gets its meaning five sections later.
- *An act with no way back*: "Run it." The page never says how to run anything.
- *Every finding, in one run*: "a flow of two steps has no exit". The declaration lists CANCEL, and how EXIT differs is never said. Also "the message reports no state": where does the declaration say so?
- *What I refused*: "The check of the promise fires only where the check of the act cannot see"; "The code carries the rule's number" (the code shown is a name); "two diagnostics which cite an article and no rule" (which two, and what was refused?).
- *Where it stands*: "its drawing for each medium is designed". To a designer, "designed" means finished. Also "hold the rest of each rule on one ladder".
- Terms never explained, or used before their explanation: verifier, law, linter, ESLint, exit code, build, JSX, intents, fixture, conformance suite, contract, diagnostic, manifest, envelope, constitution.
- Six words for what seems one thing: refusal, finding, error, violation, diagnostic, defect.

## 3. Where I doubted

- "whatever draws this declaration has no freedom to drop them". The page checks lines, and *Where it stands* puts the drawing at "designed". Nothing here holds a drawn screen to its declaration.
- "a code, a name, a rule, an article and a right", against "12 cite nothing". How are those twelve answered, when a refusal has "two answers and no third"?
- "Silence is the one answer the verifier refuses." Plate 8.4 was refused for a contradiction.
- "So a refused declaration has a lawful twin". Of 77 fixtures 28 are clean, and `multiple-violations.zayn` shows no twin.
- "on this page the verifier read three fields". The fourth finding of Plate 8.3 stands on a declaration holding only TONE.
- "confirmation interrupts always and trusts nothing", yet Rule 16 refuses a flow lacking CONFIRM. Which does the law want?
- Nothing reads as advertisement; nothing sells the work short.

## 4. Held, bored

Held: "Undo reverses what was done. Cancel abandons what is being done." Also "a user who trusts that promise learns the truth at the one moment it matters".

Bored: *The court* (thirteen rows ending "answers as the manifest expects"); the machine block of Plate 8.4.

## 5. The plates

- **8.1**: declaration understood. Report readable after the three-line reading, the best teaching on the page. The illustration helps: each missing control carries its rule.
- **8.2**: the clearest. One line added, one sentence added to "In words", a one-line report, the same surface. Its caption answered my question about placement: "Where each one sits is the drawing's business".
- **8.3**: "In words" understood. Findings one and two readable; three (EXIT) and four (an animation code on a message with a tone) not. No illustration, no repair, where I wanted both.
- **8.4**: the best "In words" line. Report readable. In the machine block I followed code, span and cites, and skipped the rest.
- **8.5**: clear. But "until the user confirms" here and "At every step" in 8.2, for the same field.
- **8.6**: understood, and I can write the codes I expect from Plate 8.3. I cannot run it, and two of those codes never had their repair shown.

## 6. Errors of form

- *What I refused*: "as the plate above shows" means Plate 8.3; the plates just above are 8.4 and 8.5.
- Rule card: "machine · a machine: the verifier refuses the declaration" doubles the word.
- Plate 8.4: the label says "The same findings", the prose "the same finding"; there is one.
- Calques: "at the release 0.2.0 of Zayn", "the criterion of WCAG".

## 7. Verdict

`ready after the items above`: the pairs 8.1/8.2 and 8.4/8.5 teach a designer to read a refusal; the vocabulary, the EXIT question and an exercise I cannot run keep me from using it.

The sentence that stopped me most: "a flow of two steps has no exit".

# Part X, In Practice: read from the educator's door

Pages, in order, read twice: chapter 25, *The command and the gate*; chapter 26, *A site under its own law*.

## Chapter 25: The command and the gate

**1. What I remember.** A build reads one number from the command: 1 when a declaration is refused (a syntax error included), 0 when none is (warnings pass), 2 when the file cannot be opened. The level and the symbol table decide what that number can stop, and plates 25.1 to 25.8 run each case on one timesheet.

**2. Where I lost the thread.**
- Terms before they are said: "suite of fixtures" and "pins" (The command, as released; said in The court and What I refused); "level 4" in the release table; "pack" three times before "The pack is the set of files a team pins".
- The door promises "every other word is said where it first appears"; SATISFIES, ALWAYS_REACHABLE, INTENT_FLOW, ROLE and the verbs READ, ACT are never said.
- "operator" in INVISIBLE_STATE's message, where the book says user.
- "the two axes of media and statuses" (What I refused); "the pack's instrument that compares two media" (Where the field leads).
- "The two files get the same answer." The same as each other, or as when checked alone?
- The court's run prints "59 diagnostics" and "40 diagnostics", and two contracts (1.11, and the Diagnostic Contract 1.2.2); the page never says which is which.

**3. Where I doubted.**
- "That is all a CI gate needs." Two sections later a level 3 gate without its table prints clean, exit 0: it also needs --symbols.

**4. What was mine.** Can a student follow the order, and is the exercise real? The order holds: I could restate the three values and what the table adds without looking. The exercise (25.9) is a constructed case, and asks for SELECT_ACT_COLLAPSE and MISSING_UNDO, which this page never names. Its answer, plate 25.10, adds approve_team_week with ACT and no ACTOR; by plate 25.5 that flow is refused at level 3 with project.json. A student who learned 25.5 adds ACTOR manager, and the page does not say why its repair leaves it out. Nothing tells a student how to get the command onto a machine to run the lab. Partly got.

**5. Verdict: ready after the items above.** The three values and the levels are taught by run; the exercise and what is undecidable need settling. Stopped me most: "undecidable is silence, never a pass", under a run that prints clean and exits 0.

## Chapter 26: A site under its own law

**1. What I remember.** The Zayn site declares its menu and eight pages, and three instruments check it: the verifier a declaration, the build's checks a file, the browser audit the painted page. Proof: plate 26.4 is clean on the commit where the editor filled 935 of 1596 pixels, which only K36, rewritten after the remark of 17 November, could see.

**2. Where I lost the thread.**
- The principle: "I had asked for that register of writing more than once". Which register? The step from "a check is trusted once it has failed" to the writing rule is not made.
- NAV_GRAPH is said after plate 26.1, not before it.
- "the lists at the side of a page of its family and its headings": I parsed it three times. "The corpus keeps each remark of mine": corpus is not said.
- Plate 26.5: "One of its pages is left out". By the excerpt, or as the defect?
- Plate 26.3's report cites Rule 119, "A Card Is Not a Button"; the page reads only the navigate part.
- The court: "Of one fact of the painted page the suite says why it keeps no fixture". Which fact?

**3. Where I doubted.**
- The principle says each remark leaves a check "written to fail on the page as it was". Every measurement in the chain is a pass (96 percent, 33 flat lists, 1596 by 930); no check is shown failing first.
- K37: "the code's pane lost 149 and the report's gained 150". Within what tolerance does that pass?

**4. What was mine.** The exercise is real: "a real defect from the site's record, of 14 November 2026". Its first right answer is clean ("If you wrote clean, you read it right"), which teaches the limit of a declaration. Then the student writes a defect on purpose (26.7) and repairs it (26.8): a seminar ending on an open question. Got it.

**5. Verdict: ready after the items above.** The chain is a usable case study; its principle is stated and not shown. Stopped me most: "each remark made on it leaves a check, written to fail on the page as it was".

## The part

**Hand-over.** Chapter 25 ends "The last chapter runs it on the Zayn site's own declarations", and 26 picks it up; 26 ends on Appendix A.

**Where the pages disagree.**
- What is undecidable gets three answers in chapter 25: a warning is "lawful but suspect, or undecidable without more input" (exit 0); "undecidable is silence, never a pass" (no finding, exit 0); the media instrument "answers 1 while a difference stays undecided".
- A name with nothing behind it: plate 25.3 warns, "An INTENT_FLOW reference names no declared flow."; plate 26.1's ENTRIES name build_family, web_family and audit_family, declared nowhere, and it is clean, with no word on why.
- One word, two things: "calls each verifier of the family a court" against "The court of this chapter is these fixtures of the suite"; "A verb is the first word after the command's name." (25) against "FOCUS, ACT and UNDO are three of the law's verbs" (26).
- "Level 4 is the painted page, the page as a browser draws it." (25), a level of the verifier; "The verifier decides a declaration." (26), and the painted page goes to a browser audit. Which instrument checks it?

**Clearest plate:** 26.4, one clean run on a page that was wrong: "the box was right, and the thing in it was not". **Hardest:** 26.5, a list written for the book, clean, whose defect the text names ambiguously.

**What my reader takes away.** Chapter 25 is a lab for one session (the three numbers, the levels, the table), once students hold SATISFIES and the verbs from earlier parts. Chapter 26 is a case study of where each instrument's sight ends, with a real exercise. Both pages show a visible [AUTHOR: ...] line; I would hand them to students once it is filled.

## The part

**Hand-over.** Chapter 1 hands over to chapter 2 ("reads the corrections whole"), and chapter 12 to chapter 13 ("crosses the media with what a user does"). The doors of 12 and 13 leave out INTENT_FLOW and levels, which a student coming from chapter 2 lacks.

**Contradictions.**
- Rule 82 sits under "Article II" (12.2) and under "Article III" (2.3, 12.8).
- Chapter 1 says "ACTOR agent names an AI agent", and chapter 13 calls `purchasing_agent` one: is ACTOR a free name?
- Chapter 1 says "123 numbered rules", and chapter 13 names Rule 125.

**Clearest plate.** 2.1 and 2.2: one word changes, and the answer goes from refused to clean. I could restate Rule 112 without looking. **Hardest:** 1.4, a code to predict whose message does not fit `navigate`.

**Visible notes.** Every page prints "[AUTHOR: the stake of this chapter…]", and chapter 1 has two such notes. A student takes them for notes left in.

**What the educator takes away.** A sequence I can teach: declare, refuse, repair, then ask what the verifier cannot judge ("Clean, and untrue"; Use and Try). Two exercises are real and sourced; the other two never say. Before I set these pages to students: say what a level, an article and INTENT_FLOW are; make the counts agree with the tables; give each exercise its source and say what a variant answer gets.

5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The hardest of the three for my door; its principle survives. Stopped most: "the forged evaluator, an evaluator that a program makes from the second declaration with no hand in between."

## The part

- **Hand-over.** Each last line hands on: "It also uses interfaces, as a person does" (22 to 23); "An AI agent that operates an interface may also write one" (23 to 24).
- **Contradictions.** Chapter 24: "chapter 22 shows the loop in which one does"; chapter 22 files that loop as designed, "a procedure a person runs". Chapter 22: HIDDEN_AUTHORITY "asks every act to name its actor"; its contract line: "A consequential act names no actor." Which acts? The prose says verb; VERB_NOT_CARRIED says "an intent the declared medium cannot carry", and the two are never said to be one thing. "navigate" is a role in 22 and a refused verb in 24, unremarked. Chapter 23's tier word, "a rule that a program is to check, with that check to come", is the definition I understood; chapter 22 needs it where the column first appears.
- **Clearest plate:** 23.7, the statement by voice: I named ZOOM before the answer. **Hardest:** 24.8.
- **Taken away.** An AI agent stands under the same rules as a person, as writer, actor and user; the verifier reads what is written, and whether the named actor is the right one is my judgment at review. A grammar holds the shape, the verifier the meaning, and "clean" means nothing found, sometimes because nothing could be decided.

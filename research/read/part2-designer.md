# Part II, The Heritage: read from the designer's door

Reader: the designer who does not program. Pages read twice, in order: chapter 3, chapter 4, chapter 5. Date: 2026-11-25.

## Chapter 3, What the field wrote

1. **What I remember.** The heuristics I already use (visibility of status, undo, error prevention, consistency) each have a numbered rule in Zayn's law, and where a declaration can state the rule, the verifier refuses the file by name. The proof is four refusals and their repairs, and a field for target size that the language does not have, because size belongs to the drawing.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "One meaning for each status": "The law files Rule 118 under judgment, and the refusal judges a word of the list". A rule filed for a person to judge, refused by a machine: I could not reconcile the two.
   - The map table against the sentence under it. The table gives "Rule 16 judgment" and "Rule 51 judgment (Article II)", then: "The verifier checks Rules 82, 79, 26, 51 and 16 by name". After the chapter's own definition of a tier ("for the instrument that owes its check"), I expected judgment rules not to be checked by the verifier. One sentence is missing that says a tier names who owes the whole rule, while the verifier can still check one part of it.
   - "Interest before commitment": "a reader who looks for error prevention in Rule 16's text finds it in the article above". I had to read it three times.
   - Terms used before they were said: **level 4** ("Both run clean at level 4", under "Where the field leads"; the page only explained levels 2 and 3); **the record** ("The record draws two links itself"; never said what the record is); **level 1** (in the court's code list, "UNKNOWN_SIGNAL level 1", while the plates ran at level 2).
3. **Where I doubted.** Plate 3.2: the same file runs clean when the table is missing. The page says so honestly, but a designer reading "Clean" may take it as approval; one sentence on who supplies the table in a real team would help here and not only in chapter 25.
4. **What was mine.** The map from the heuristics I know to the law's rules. I got it: I could say from memory that undo is Rule 79 and status words are Rule 118.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** The tier and the verifier's checks seem to contradict each other. The sentence that stopped me most: "The law files Rule 118 under judgment, and the refusal judges a word of the list".

## Chapter 4, The instruments of the field

1. **What I remember.** My tools (tokens, Storybook, linters, screenshot tests, layout-shift scores) each read the drawing; a declaration reads what the user can do, so two cards drawn alike can keep different promises. The proof is the dashboard's two cards: one word, ROLE, turns the verdict, and no pixel changes.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "The law names its instruments": "The field is lint, and 18 rules carry it." Up to here, "the field" has meant my discipline. Here it means a slot in the rule's data. "That field is the bridge between a rule and the field's instruments" uses the word in both senses in one sentence. This is the worst stop in the part.
   - Same section: "Softanza's graphics plane", "a manifest lint", "the profile a project's manifest names". None of them is explained.
   - "Two cards drawn alike": "It also cites Rule 112, on motion". Why a rule on motion for a card that does not move? The quoted reason did not answer it for me.
   - "The field": "the impact fraction of D multiplied by the distance fraction of D". This is opaque to me; the plain threshold that follows it is enough.
   - "CSS pixels", used before it is said.
3. **Where I doubted.** "So a clean report at level 4 says that the declaration keeps the law." Chapter 3 told me that two surfaces with "seven broken rules" both "run clean at level 4". The two sentences fit together only if I hold on to "the declaration"; a hurried reader of this sentence hears "the interface is lawful". Also, the first item under "What I refused" is a move ("I moved a menu's structure"), not a refusal.
4. **What was mine.** Where my design system stops and the declaration starts. I got it, fully: "The two cards are one drawing and two promises."
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** "field" means two things. The sentence that stopped me most: "The field is lint, and 18 rules carry it."

## Chapter 5, The road sign

1. **What I remember.** The road sign is an interface fixed by a treaty: a closed set of signs a stranger can read at speed, changed by its parties on a memorandum. Zayn does the same with a closed list of media, verbs and status words; a sign that promises to act, or asks to be read, is refused.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - Plate 5.1: every refusal of a verb a sign cannot carry cites "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article II". Why does opening a gate fall under perceiving state? This is not said.
   - The promise line "//--> 4 VERB_NOT_CARRIED": the count before the code is not explained.
   - Terms: **hash** ("its edition is pinned by hash"); **the platform** ("founding document for the platform"); **the record**, again.
3. **Where I doubted.** "The principle": "My outline of this book's second part describes it in one line". Showing the claim and then cutting it to its true size is honest, but the book's own outline and ledger are workshop material, and they pull me away from the sign.
4. **What was mine.** Proof that a closed visual vocabulary can be law and still work across languages. I got it, and the exit sign exercise ("the word on the sign and the verb of the law are two things") taught me the difference between a label and an intent.
5. **Verdict: ready after the items above.** Rule 82 is cited for no reason I can see. The sentence that stopped me most: "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article II".

# Part VI, read from the door of the decision-maker who commissions software

Pages read, in order, twice: chapter 14, *Two cultures, one law*; chapter 15, *Culture as an axis*. The part as given holds two pages, not four.

## Chapter 14: Two cultures, one law

**What I remember.** A supplier may restyle an interface (a "culture": palette, type, density), but a culture that hides a verb the flow promises, such as undo, is refused by the verifier. Proof: Plate 14.2, where Retro suppresses UNDO and gets VIBE_STRIPS_RIGHT, exit code 1.

**Where I lost the thread.**
- "The law's notes name them curator and guardian." (Two cultures, one site): two new names, never used again; I do not know whether they matter to me.
- "The site's exhibit draws one screen of operations" (same section): what the exhibit is, and where, is never said.
- Terms used before they were said: "level 2" (every report; levels are never explained on the page), "the court's fixture" (said at "The court", after its first use), "the docket" (What I refused), "tier" and "judgment" (The law a reader's language keeps, said only in passing), "the audit of the painted page".
- What I refused: the quotes on "that ugly blue" and green checkboxes are the author's remarks on his own site; I could not tell what they change in a project I pay for.

**Where I doubted.**
- "Zayn leads in what a culture may never touch." Two sentences later: "a culture kept in a file of its own answers clean beside a flow in another". That is the gap I would most need to know before writing a contract, and it sits at the end of *Where the field leads*, after the claim. A supplier who puts its theme in its own file passes the check.
- Plate 14.6 commentary: "Rule 112, on motion, is a person's to judge." Yet Plate 14.4's report shows the verifier refusing DECORATIVE_ANIMATION and citing Rule 112. Is Rule 112 a machine check or a person's? As written, both.

**What was mine.** What I can require of a supplier: declare every culture beside the flow it draws, in the same file, and accept a clean report at level 2 as proof no culture drops a declared way back. I got it, but I had to assemble the "same file" condition myself. Cost of adoption and when the check runs (each delivery? each build?) are not said.

**Verdict: ready after the items above.** The refusal is shown and useful; the one-file limit must stand beside the claim. Sentence that stopped me most: "a culture kept in a file of its own answers clean beside a flow in another".

## Chapter 15: Culture as an axis

**What I remember.** A declaration may carry a meaning (success, danger) but never a colour; what a colour means to a reader is left to the rendering per culture, which is designed, not built. Proof: Plate 15.1 refuses "green, red" twice; Plate 15.3 shows a flag-labelled language menu passing clean although the law forbids it.

**Where I lost the thread.**
- "what The Second Founding, a proposal of the project for a new base of the law, designs" (opening): a proposal's name arrives before I know whose or how binding.
- "the tier of a rule whose instrument is to come" (What the law holds): "machine-later" and "instrument" are new; I took "instrument" to mean a check that does not exist.
- "in Rule 118's second family, the quiet one" (In your hands): the page lists five statuses but never says Rule 118 has two families.
- "stratum" (Three questions about a sign) relies on chapter 11; fine as a pointer, but the sentence needs it now.

**Where I doubted.**
- "already culture-safe on that axis by construction": the page shows only that a colour word in SIGNALS is refused. The claim is about one field; the quote reads wider.
- "The verifier decides one thing on this axis: the colour word" — the court then lists SIGNAL_UNPERCEIVABLE as well. Two things, or one?
- The summary: "a person judges Rules 64 and 122" — chapter 14 and this page's own *Who decides* put Rule 63 under judgment too.

**What was mine.** What is checked by machine and what I must staff: Plate 15.3 told me exactly that a flag-labelled menu passes the verifier, so a person's review under Rule 122 must be in the acceptance plan. I got it, clearly. Rule 56 ("binds once a meaning-to-rendering path exists") told me I cannot require it today. What a review by a person costs, and who that person is, is not said.

**Verdict: ready after the items above.** The split between machine and person is the clearest thing in the part. Sentence that stopped me most: "Clean, and the law forbids it."

## For the part

**Hand-over.** Chapter 14 ends on "the culture the reader brings", and chapter 15 opens on it and marks the two senses of "culture" at once. Clean hand-over. Chapter 15 closes into Part VII.

**Contradictions.**
- Ch14: "Rule 112, on motion, is a person's to judge." / Ch14 Plate 14.4: DECORATIVE_ANIMATION "Rule 112 · Motion Reports, It Never Decorates".
- Ch14: "The law files all three under judgment" (63, 64, 122). / Ch15: "a person judges Rules 64 and 122".
- Ch15 court: "UNKNOWN_SIGNAL level 1" / every report: "at level 2". Without levels explained, I read these as disagreeing.

**Clearest plate.** 15.3: clean, and forbidden. It tells me in one run where the machine stops and my review starts.

**Hardest plate.** 14.6: it is accepted, the house forbids it, and the rule it touches is said to be a person's, after the verifier cited it.

**What I take away.** I can require that every culture sit in the same file as its flow and pass level 2; I must budget a person to review labels, direction and language; Rule 56 and the reader's culture are not yet contractable. Neither page tells me what adoption costs or when in a project the check is run.

## The three items that most need fixing

1. Ch14: put the one-file limit of VIBE_STRIPS_RIGHT beside the claim that the verifier refuses a culture that hides a way back, so a buyer can write "cultures in the flow's file" into a requirement.
2. Ch14: reconcile "Rule 112 is a person's to judge" with the verifier's DECORATIVE_ANIMATION citing Rule 112 (say which part the machine checks).
3. Both: say in a clause what "level" means (level 1 vs level 2) and what "tier", "judgment" and "machine-later" mean, and align the list of rules a person judges (63, 64, 122).

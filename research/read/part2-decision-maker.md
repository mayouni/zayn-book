# Part II, The Heritage: read from the decision-maker's door

Reader: the decision-maker who commissions software. Pages read twice, in order: chapters 3, 4, 5 (as-read text of 2026-11-25).

## Chapter 3, What the field wrote

1. **What I remember.** The usability heuristics I already pay reviewers to apply (feedback, undo, error prevention, consistency) sit in the law as numbered rules; where a declaration can state them, the verifier refuses by name before anything is drawn. Proof: four refusals run and repaired, then a map of which rules a machine checks and which a person judges.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "The record draws two links itself" (The map): "the record" is never said on this page. Also "design note", "lineage", "the second founding" arrive bare.
   - "Both run clean at level 4" (Where the field leads): level 4 is not said in this chapter; only levels 2 and 3 are.
   - "The law files Rule 118 under judgment, and the refusal judges a word of the list" (One meaning): a judgment-tier rule refused by a machine. I came to learn which rules I can require a machine to check; the tier word stops being my guide here.
3. **Where I doubted.**
   - The map says "Rule 26 machine-later" and "Rule 51 judgment", then "The verifier checks Rules 82, 79, 26, 51 and 16 by name". Machine-later was defined as "the tier of a rule whose instrument comes later". If the verifier checks Rule 26 today, what does machine-later mean in a contract?
   - "What Zayn adds, in the site's words: 'Refusal.'" reads as a slogan.
4. **What was mine.** Plate 3.2: the same file runs clean at level 2 when no table is given. That tells me a supplier must hand over the symbol table and the run must be at level 3, or the check is silent. I got it, but only by inference; the page points to chapter 25 instead of saying "require level 3 with the table". Also mine and got: two payment surfaces "differ by seven broken rules" yet run clean. Clean is not good, and the page says so.
5. **Verdict:** ready after the items above. A clean map of the field to the law; the tiers need one sentence on what each tier means for who checks it now. Stopped me most: "Rule 26 machine-later" set beside "The verifier checks Rules 82, 79, 26, 51 and 16 by name".

## Chapter 4, The instruments of the field

1. **What I remember.** Every tool my suppliers already run (WCAG checks, tokens, Storybook, a linter, screenshot tests, layout-shift scores) reads the drawing; a declaration reads what a tap does, so a drawing that passes them all can still be refused. Proof: a checkout where choosing pays, and two cards drawn alike, one of which acts.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "The field is lint, and 18 rules carry it" (The law names its instruments): "field" here is a data field, one line after "the field's own kind of instrument" meant the discipline. I read it three times.
   - "K22 LAYOUT_STILL", "K17 TARGET": what K is, unsaid.
   - "Softanza's graphics plane", "a manifest lint ... the profile a project's manifest names": bare.
3. **Where I doubted.**
   - "Each decides on its object, and each object can be in order": asserted, not run. No token, lint or screenshot check is shown passing. Stated as "can", so acceptable, but the chapter's whole case rests on it.
   - "So a clean report at level 4 says that the declaration keeps the law." Chapter 3 said "a person judges the others on the painted page", and this chapter says LAYOUT_DISPLACEMENT is at level 4 yet measured by the audit. A clean level-4 run keeps the rules the verifier checks, not the law. In a contract that sentence would be over-read.
4. **What was mine.** "do not cite WCAG, and this page does not claim they implement it": I cannot accept Zayn in place of WCAG conformance. And the audit measures "a much narrower set than axe-core's". I got both, and they are exactly what a buyer needs. Missing: one sentence of what to require of a supplier: the verifier's report, the audit's run, and the field's own instruments, each for its object.
5. **Verdict:** ready after the items above. The two-cards pair makes the case in one word. Stopped me most: "So a clean report at level 4 says that the declaration keeps the law."

## Chapter 5, The road sign

1. **What I remember.** Road signs are an interface fixed by a treaty: a closed vocabulary, amended by its parties on memorandum and experiment. Zayn does the same with closed lists (media, verbs a medium carries, five status meanings), and the verifier refuses what falls outside: a sign that promises to act, a medium not on the list, a sixth meaning.
2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article II" (Plate 5.1): why a sign that cannot open a gate breaks perceivable state is not said.
   - "its edition is pinned by hash" (Where the field leads): hash unexplained, and it is the one sentence I would put in a contract.
3. **Where I doubted.**
   - Contradiction: chapter 3 prints "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article III · the right to know"; chapter 5 prints "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article II". One rule, two articles.
   - "Zayn's law binds whoever adopts it ... its author rules its amendments": the treaty binds 75 parties; this law is amended by one author. Stated honestly, but a buyer will ask who rules if the author does not; the page leaves that to chapter 18.
4. **What was mine.** Governance of the vocabulary: pinned editions, amendments on evidence, a designed tier where an external standard "outranks this constitution". I got the shape; I did not get what I can require (pin which edition, how a change reaches my project).
5. **Verdict:** ready after the items above. The sign-versus-print pair (5.3, 5.4) is the clearest proof in the part. Stopped me most: "Rule 82 · Perceivable State · Article II" under a sign that cannot act.

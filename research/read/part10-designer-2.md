# Part X, In Practice: the designer who does not program, second reading

## Chapter 25: The command and the gate

1. **Remembered.** The gate reads one number. A refusal stops the build with 1, and so does a file the verifier cannot parse. A warning still gives 0, so the build goes on and the warning stays in the log. 2 means the command was handed something it could not read. At level 3 the verifier needs the project's table of actors and state: without the table it says nothing, and the gate passes. Each file is judged on its own. The proof is Plate 25.5 and the command line under it: the same week refused twice with the table, and "clean" at level 3 without it.

2. **Lost the thread.**

Yet the page says what a level is only in the last section: "A level is how much the verifier is given." In the same way, "the machine form for a tool" is used before "A tool reads the same refusal in the machine form, with the option --json".
   - "it answers one verb, check": earlier, "SATISFIES lists the law's verbs that the user performs there" (READ, ACT). "Verb" now means a word typed after the command's name. One chapter gives one word two meanings.
   - "pack writes or checks the manifest of the files a team pins, that is, records by their hash": "manifest" is explained only in What I refused, and "hash" thirteen lines later. "audit-facts reads the facts of the audit of screenshots" is never explained: which audit, which screenshots?

   - "the record names its computation from the project as the work that follows": I read this twice before I understood it to mean that the table will one day be computed rather than written by hand.

## The part

**Hand-over.** Yes. Chapter 25 closes on "The last chapter runs it on the Zayn site's own declarations, a site held to the law it publishes.", and chapter 26 opens on "A site under its own law is a site that publishes a law for interfaces and holds its own pages to it." Chapter 26 mostly closes the book well. "The law is printed in full in Appendix A, from its data, for the reader who will hold a site of their own to it." turns the book toward my own site, and that is the right last line. But both chapters end their exercise on a question the page does not answer ("Who reads that difference before the change ships?" and "Should a declaration be able to say that every page it names is reached by a menu"). And both have an [AUTHOR] hole where the stake should be, so the part ends without saying why.

**Where the pages disagree.**
- Chapter 25: "which the family calls a court" (a language with its law and verifier). Chapter 26: "The court of this chapter is these fixtures of the suite". Chapter 25 also uses the second sense.
- Chapter 25: a flow that a form names and no file declares gets a warning, "An INTENT_FLOW reference names no declared flow." Chapter 26: "An entry that names no declared NAV_GRAPH is not judged, since its label is not in the file." In both cases a name is missing, yet one gets a warning and the other gets nothing.
- Chapter 25: "a run at level 4 runs every check the verifier holds" and "here is its run at level 4, which takes in every fixture". Chapter 26: "The suite keeps no fixture for the one kind of finding at level 4". So the run at level 4 takes in no fixture of level 4, and neither page says what level 4 adds for the verifier.
- Chapter 25: "undecidable is silence, never a pass" (0 at level 3 without a table) and, in its last sections, "a difference stays undecided, and the run answers 1". Chapter 26: "Undecidable from a declaration: it is a fact about a painted page." Three undecided cases end three different ways.

**Clearest plate / Hardest plate.** Clearest: Plate 26.7. The report line quotes "Design system" twice, in the words a visitor would see. I could predict the refusal from the rule given at the start of the chapter, and Plate 26.8's repair changes one label. Hardest: Plate 26.5. The defect is visible in the declaration, yet the verifier says clean. The page calls the level the defect and then calls a menu with levels lawful, before sending the judgment to K34 on the painted page, without saying whose rule a flat side list is.

**What I take away.** A refusal stops a team's build through one number, a warning lets the build go, and 2 means the gate was set wrong. What the verifier can decide depends on what it is given: without the table it stays silent and the gate passes, and it reads each file on its own, so where I put a flow and its screen changes the answer. A declaration says what a user does and never how large or where things are drawn. Sizes, popups and side lists belong to the browser audit, and a check is trusted once it has failed on the thing itself rather than its box. In a menu, the landing page comes first, a child never repeats its parent's label, and no two siblings share a label.

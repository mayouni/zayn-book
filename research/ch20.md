## 1. What the chapter does

It shows the loop from a remark to a rule as a practice, on real records of `doc/design/corpus/corpus.json`: what a record keeps
(its fields), how a repeat is marked and what the mark proves, how records are gathered (the sweeps), how a record that does not
quote the author is kept apart as a candidate, and which records stand behind which rule of the law. It follows three records
from their words to the rule and, where one exists, to a check or a fixture: a toggle on a book reader (Rule 106, amended), the
smooth scroll of a reader (Rule 112, to `DECORATIVE_ANIMATION` and its fixtures, and the same defect corrected twice afterwards
in other projects), and the size of type (Rule 105, to the audit of the painted page, and a proposed amendment the corpus
argues for). It ends on the capture that the Second Founding designs for every application: the remark taken where the pain
happens, with its surface, its intent and its medium. Chapters 2, 18 and 19 have told the counts, the menu remark and how a rule
is written (section 13 lists what they hold); chapter 20 tells the practice that feeds them.

## 4. The principle

The record returns to Article VII: "The law grows only from evidence: real usage, operator feedback, documented failure
analysis." (`constitution/rules.json:55`), and to the design study's reading of the corpus: "A correction stored as a symptom does
not stop the defect; it moves it." (`DESIGN-SYSTEM.md:35`, already the principle's source in chapters 2 and 19). The loop's own
sentence is the founding's: "The person who feels the pain has never had a channel to the person who decides."
(`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:822`).

Proposed bold sentence, in the book's words: **A remark becomes evidence when it is kept with its words, its place, its date
and its cause, and a rule is written when the same remark comes back.** (Alternative, nearer the contract: *A person's pain
reaches the law only as a record: the words, where, when, why, and whether it came before.*)

## 11. Terms a designer who does not program needs

- **Correction / remark**: what a person says about something they see, touch or hear on an interface; the corpus's unit
  (`CORPUS.md:5`).
- **Record**: one correction as data, with its 14 fields (`corpus.json`).
- **Verbatim**: the record keeps the author's words "typing kept"; otherwise `[paraphrase]` (`CORPUS.md:5`).
- **Repeat**: the record's source names an earlier correction of the same defect (`CORPUS.md:5`).
- **Root cause**: "the default that produced the defect: the thing the generator does when nothing stops it."
  (`DESIGN-SYSTEM.md:122-123`).
- **Sweep**: one read-only pass over a set of files that gathers records; each record keeps the id it had in its sweep
  (`CORPUS.md:9`; `corpus.json` `sweeps`).
- **Relay (`also`)**: the same remark found again in another file, listed under the record (`CORPUS.md:5`).
- **Candidate (in the corpus)**: a record that looks like an answered remark but whose source does not quote or name the author,
  kept apart and uncounted (`render.js:68`). Not the docket's "candidate rule" (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:1072`): say which.
- **Ruling (field)**: the decision a record led to, by its id: a site's ruling or a rule of the law (`corpus.json`).
- **Docket / pass**: the file where changes to the law are proposed and ruled item by item (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:1`).
- **Lineage**: the dated history of the law inside its own file (`rules.json:10`).
- **Capture**: designed: taking a remark in the application at the moment it hurts, with "the surface, the intent it served, the
  medium it was in" (`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:811-812`).

## 1. What the chapter does

It tells, at its true size, how the law's rules are enforced. The law files every rule under one word in its `enforcement`
field (`machine`, `tool`, `machine-later`, `judgment`), and three instruments exist in the repository: the verifier, which
refuses a declaration; the audits, which measure a painted page (or the facts taken from a picture of one); and a person, who
judges. The chapter shows which rule each instrument reaches today, counted by citation with the definition of the count
printed beside it, next to the tier the law files the rule under, and it says what a tier means in the record's own words.
It runs one rule the verifier decides from an inventory (Rule 130), one it decides in part (Rule 79), tells one audit check
from its recorded run, and shows one rule that a person alone judges (Rule 102). It is on the proposed spine, so it reads alone.

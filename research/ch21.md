## 1. What the chapter does

It takes the three tiers of law that *The Second Founding* v0.4 designs, and the fourth above them: statute (an external
instrument that outranks the law), the core (what every adopter keeps: preamble, seven articles, the rights, the intent families'
invariants, the forbidden patterns), the commons (rules contributed and admitted by evidence, today's rules among them) and the
local (a person's or an organisation's own laws, "pinned alongside, shipped with the app"). It says who may add to each, how a
contribution is admitted (the admission test "already in the data", `derived_from`; the admission triple: gotcha, fixture,
evidence), and the boundary the proposal draws: "What is non-negotiable is what is true of human beings. What is contributed is
what is true of a place." Then it shows what a project holds **today**: a pin by hash on twelve files (`pack.js --check`), a
diagnostic contract it can supply (`--contract`), cultures it names itself (`VIBE`, a free name and a free `DENSITY`), and the one
place the language already keeps a place's choice from loosening the core (`VIBE_STRIPS_RIGHT`). Chapter 20 takes the corpus loop
(a remark becomes a rule); chapter 21 takes the tiers, the admission and the pin.

## 4. The principle

The record returns to one sentence, written for culture and carried into governance: "What is non-negotiable is what is true of
human beings. What is contributed is what is true of a place." (`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:449-450`, again `:788-791`). Its
operative companion is the supremacy rule: "a local law may be *stricter* than the commons and never *looser*"
(`:785`). The data already holds the admission half: every rule derives from an article (`derived_from`, counted).

Proposed bold sentence, in the book's words: **What is true of every person is kept by every team; what is true of one place is
added by that place, and it may only add.** (Shorter alternative: *A team may add to the law; it may not take away.*) The second
clause is the design; the page says so (section 9).

## 11. Terms a designer who does not program needs

- **Statute (tier 0)**: an external law or binding standard over a medium (road signage, machine indicators), which "outranks
  this constitution absolutely" (`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:771-777`).
- **Core**: the part of the law every adopter keeps, "Amended only by convention, never by contribution" (`:778-779`).
- **Commons**: the rules contributed and admitted on evidence, today's among them (`:780-781`).
- **Local**: a person's or an organisation's own laws, "pinned alongside, shipped with the app" (`:782-783`).
- **Supremacy**: the proposed rule between tiers, "stricter … never looser" (`:785-786`).
- **Admission test**: a proposed rule must derive from an article and contradict no part of the core; the rest is evidence
  (`:793-796`).
- **Admission triple**: the three forms a contributed rule arrives in: a gotcha a person can feel, a fixture a machine can check,
  evidence the record can hold (`:798-806`).
- **Gotcha**: an offense and its lawful twin, playable on the site (`:804`; `site/gotchas/`).
- **Pin**: the version of the law a project records as the one it is held to; "Re-pinning is its own act" (`AMENDMENT-DOCKET.md:104-107`).
- **Hash**: a number computed from a file's bytes; "A hash says the bytes you have are that edition" (`protocol/README.md:48-49`).
- **Vendor**: to copy the pack's files into one's own project (`protocol/README.md:51`).
- **Contract (the diagnostic contract)**: the file that gives each kind of finding its severity, level and citation, which the
  verifier loads (`cli.js:19-22`); a project may supply its own (`--contract`).
- **Vibe / culture of interaction**: a declared way a place uses the interface (density, medium, motion), Article VI's sense
  (`rules.json:47-49`; `DECLARATIONS.md:196-210`).
- **Precedence**: a rule that says which of two conflicting rules wins; the law "has no precedence mechanism at all"
  (`EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md:221`).

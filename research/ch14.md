## 1. What the chapter does

It takes what is built of culture: an interaction culture, which the law calls a vibe, is how one interface presents itself to
a community of users, and the law (Article VI) lets it change the look and never the rights. Two cultures are built on the Zayn
site, Modern and Retro (the law's `curator` and `guardian`), as one set of token names with two sets of values, and the site's
exhibit (`site/vibes.html`) draws one screen in both at once from one shared state. In the language a culture is a declaration,
`VIBE`, and the verifier refuses a culture that hides an intent another declaration guarantees (`VIBE_STRIPS_RIGHT`); the chapter
runs that refusal and its lawful twin, two cultures over one flow, and the fields a culture may not carry. It names Set 18 (Rules
63, 64, 122: dialect, direction, language as data) as the law that a reader's language keeps, and the forbidden pattern bound to
Rule 122. Culture as an axis (the reading of a sign by a people, `CONVENTION`, Rule 56's cultural clause, A-7) is chapter 15's.

## 4. The principle

The record returns to one sentence, the last of Article VI, carried word for word into the contract as the invariant of
`VIBE_STRIPS_RIGHT`: "Vibe-invariant rights never flex." (`constitution/rules.json:49`; `conformance/violations.json:251`). Its

no freedom over *what* the screen shows" (`site/vibes.html:141-143`).

Proposed bold sentence, in the book's words: **A culture changes how an interface looks to its users; it never changes what the
interface lets them do.** (Shorter alternative: *A culture may restyle the way back; it may never remove it.*)

## 11. Terms a designer who does not program needs

- **Culture (interaction culture)**: how one interface presents itself to a community of users; the law's word for it is
  *vibe* (`site/vibes.html:91-93`; `rules.json:45-49`; `EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md:89`).
- **Vibe**: the law's and the language's name for an interaction culture; `DEFINE VIBE` declares one (`DECLARATIONS.md:196`).
- **Modern / Retro**: the Zayn site's two cultures, the law's `curator` and `guardian` (`site/vibes.html:74-77`; `site/stz.css:22,54`).
- **Token**: a named value the stylesheet uses; a *fixed* token holds in both cultures, a *culture* token is set by each
  (`DESIGN-SYSTEM.md:596-597`).
- **Guarantee (`ALWAYS_REACHABLE`)**: a verb a declaration promises stays within reach of the user (`DECLARATIONS.md:210`).
- **Suppress (`SUPPRESSES`)**: what a culture hides; lawful only for verbs nobody guarantees (`DECLARATIONS.md:210`).
- **Vibe-invariant**: what holds in every culture; Article VI's "Vibe-invariant rights never flex", and the badge the law's
  reader prints on a rule marked `invariant` (`rules.json:49`; `constitution/reader/index.html:474-475`; chapter 16 introduces it).
- **Dialect**: a module's own words for the same thing ("Client", "Debtor"), Rule 63 (`rules.json:1866`).
- **Right-to-left (RTL)**: scripts read from the right, Arabic and Hebrew; Rule 64 mirrors the layout for them (`rules.json:1881`).
- **Forbidden pattern**: a named defect the law forbids, bound to the rules it violates (`rules.json:309-313`; chapter 16).

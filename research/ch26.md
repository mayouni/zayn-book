## 1. What the chapter does

It takes the Zayn site as the worked case of an interface governed under the law it publishes. It shows the site's own
declarations, the menu and eight pages that declare themselves, and runs them. It shows the three instruments the site's build
runs on itself: the verifier on its declarations, the checks on its files (`site-shell`, `site-facts`, `site-grid`), and the audit
of the painted page (K01 to K37); and it follows one chain of six remarks of the author's, 15 to 17 November 2026, from his
sentence to the check that holds it, including the remark that found a check measuring the wrong box. It ends on what a
declaration of the site states and what only the painted page or a person decides. Readable alone (the spine).

## 4. The principle

The sentence the record returns to, in the site's writing rule: "It drifted back each time because it lived only in a promise, so

landmarks written by hand would drift" (`tools/site-shell.js:14`); "A typed number that goes stale is then a failing check, not a

ledger's reason for keeping its own corrections: "a project that argues about drift and hides its own errors has refuted itself."
(`doc/EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md:24-25`).

Proposed bold sentence, in the book's words (chapter 19's principle is about a remark becoming a rule; this one is about a site
held to its law day by day):

- **A site that publishes a law answers to it first: its pages declare themselves, its build checks them, and each remark made on it leaves a check that fails on the page as it was.**
- shorter: **A law is proved first on its own site, where every remark leaves a check behind.**

What it means, for the writer: three instruments and a reader, each with its part (the declaration for what a user does; the
files for what the build writes; the paint for what a browser draws; a person for what no counter reads), and the worked case shows
a remark landing on the instrument that can decide it.

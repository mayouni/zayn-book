## 1. What the chapter does

It takes the third axis that *The Second Founding* v0.4 adds beside the strata and the media: culture, what an expression
"means to the person receiving it" (`doc/THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:7-8`). It separates what the proposal designs (the
`CONVENTION` word, "the naming is the author's", the reading of a signal per culture, variance against hazard, "a locale
formats, a culture interprets", the commons as the only source of a reading) from what the law already holds as data since the
author's pass of 2026-08-13: Rule 56's cultural clause (A-7, effective on a condition), Rule 64's list of what must not mirror
(A-8), Rule 122 and the first cultural forbidden pattern, *a flag is not a language* (A-6). It runs the one place the language
itself keeps a culture's reading out of a declaration: `SIGNALS` takes a meaning and refuses a colour word. And it says plainly
which of these a machine decides (the colour word, at the verifier) and which a person judges (Rules 63, 64, 122, all
`judgment`; Rule 56 `machine-later`). Chapter 14 takes the two built cultures of interaction (Modern and Retro, `VIBE`,
`VIBE_STRIPS_RIGHT`, Article VI); chapter 15 takes culture as the reader's reading.

## 4. The principle

The record returns to one sentence, in the founding and again in the language's own manual and in Rule 56: "declare the
meaning once, and every medium and every culture renders it." (`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:404-405`, again `:537`; `STZSENSE.md:25-26`;
in the law's words, "what a declaration fixes is the meaning, and the rendering is derived per theme, per medium and per
culture", `rules.json:1742`). Its companion for the hazard: "A sign that denotes sovereignty, faith or political identity must
never be conscripted as a label for something else." (`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:421-422`).

Proposed bold sentence, in the book's words: **A declaration fixes what a sign means; how a reader reads it belongs to the
reader's culture, and some signs are never borrowed at all.** (Shorter alternative: *The meaning is declared once; the reading
is the reader's.*)

## 11. Terms a designer who does not program needs

- **Axis (of expression)**: one of three independent questions about an expression: what it does (the stratum), where it is
  carried (the medium), what it means to its receiver (culture) (`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:7-8`).
- **Culture, in the two senses of the record**: an interaction culture, a vibe, Article VI's sense (`rules.json:49`, chapter 14);
  and the reader's reading of a sign, the third axis, for which `CONVENTION` is proposed (`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:377-381`).
- **Convention**: the proposed word for how a culture reads a sign; "Language is data; meaning is convention." (`:375`).
- **Locale**: the identifier under which a thing is written down for a place (formats, dictionary); Rule 122 adds a language by
  "registering a locale" (`rules.json:1896-1906`); "A locale formats. A culture interprets." (`:473`).
- **Reading**: what a sign means to a given receiver; it "must be contributed" (`THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:475`).
- **Variance / hazard**: a meaning that renders differently per culture, and can be parameterised; a sign that must not be used
  at all as a label, and cannot be (`:409,415`).
- **Forbidden pattern**: an entry of the law's list of named defects, each bound to the rules it violates (`rules.json` `forbidden`).
- **Mirroring**: reversing the layout's horizontal axis for a right-to-left script; Rule 64 lists what stays as it is
  (`rules.json:1888`).
- **Right-to-left (RTL)**: scripts written from right to left, Arabic and Hebrew in Rule 64's words (`rules.json:1888`).

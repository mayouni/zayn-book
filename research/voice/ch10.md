# Voice reading: chapter 10, What a declaration cannot see

Reader: a fresh voice reader (brief `writing-framework/briefs/voice-reader.md`), 2026-11-26, on `.cache/as-read/ch10-what-a-declaration-cannot-see.txt` and its source. Calibrated on chapter 4. Nothing run, nothing changed.

## 1. Shape (C9)

Opening: first sentence says what a declaration sees; then "In this chapter I draw the line where its sight ends", a four-step first-person roadmap. Six sentences. Kept.
The door paragraph says "four words come first" and defines six: declaration, law, verifier, finding, drawing, painted page (*opening*). Chapter 4 defines four.
Section closes: *One declaration, two drawings* ("The seven faults sit past it.") and *One rule, two halves* ("…and the audit reads it.") hand over well. *The line moves* closes on a verdict. Two close on a summary: *The field* ("So the field describes an interface free of any medium…") and *Where it stands* ("So a clean declaration says…"). *What reads the drawing* closes on an absence: "the audit names no check for the place of an undo."
Chapter close: a bridge to chapter 11. Its first sentence is an absence line: "things the language has no word for".

## 2. Run, then read (C1)

The declarations are run, with the refusal before the repair (10.3, 10.4). The half the chapter is about, the drawing, gets no run. *What reads the drawing* has a table of 37 checks and then a pairing of the seven faults to checks. No recorded audit run of either drawing is shown. The book's own rule is that a browser-only run is shown "from a recorded run, with its date, widths and culture". The worst paragraph:
- "So the seven faults each have a reader. K16 SHAPE reads a card that reacts under a link." (*What reads the drawing*): an assertion where a reading of the audit belongs.
Second: "Each check reads one property of every element that has it. Grouped by what they read, they cover the ground the declaration leaves" (*same*).

## 3. The defect (C2)

*In your hands* is a real defect: an irreversible send that still offers an undo. It has two lawful repairs, and the bank decides between them. The closing question hands the drawing's half to the reader too: "how will the drawing show it?" Wrong before right: 10.3 before 10.4. The clean 10.1 before the faulty 10.2 is deliberate and works. Close to chapter 8's ground (Rule 79's escape clause). The marker asks whether it is the defect he wants. No correct code is rejected for taste.

## 6. Metaphors (C5)

- **The line**, with **sight** (title, "where its sight ends", "past it", "inside them", "The line moves"): this is the chapter's ruling figure, and it carries weight. It is mapped late ("The line between the two objects is a ruling", in section six). Map it in the opening.
- "how far the hand must go": load-bearing, one job.
- "nowhere to stand in it", "cover the ground the declaration leaves": decorative.
- "rules moved up the enforcement ladder" (*Where it stands*): unmapped on this page. The register keeps the ladder for chapter 17.
- The court and the law are mapped at use.

## 8. Rhythm and noise (C7, §4)

## 11. Most him, least him

Most him:
- "Clean again, and it must be. The declaration says that an undo is within reach, and the drawing put it in a menu." (*One declaration, two drawings*)
- "The declaration says it is within reach, and the drawing decides how far the hand must go." (*One rule, two halves*)
- "Both clean. Which is true depends on the bank: whether it can recall a payment is a fact of the system, and the declaration states it." (*In your hands*)

Least him: "Zayn leads in the division. The field keeps its abstract interface as a model and its drawn product as the object of conformance. Zayn binds both to one law" (*Where the field leads*). It is all abstract nouns with no run in reach, and its reach goes past what the page shows of the audit.

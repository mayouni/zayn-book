Reader: a fresh voice reader (brief `writing-framework/briefs/voice-reader.md`), 2026-11-25. Nothing was run; nothing in the chapter was changed.

## 1. Shape (C9)

The opening keeps the shape. Its first sentence names the subject ("The road sign is an interface held by law"), and it says what the chapter does in the first person: "In this chapter I read that treaty at its source and say what it carries." The second paragraph is a four-word glossary, so the first plate is still a long way off.

Section closes. The Field ends with a summary: "So the field holds one interface whose signs are fixed in a text..." The Principle hands over ("the record makes it in data"). Both run sections end on a verdict that hands over ("A sixth meaning is an amendment of the law, as a new sign is an amendment of the treaty."). The court ends with a list of fragments, which is a dead end.

The chapter ends with a bridge ("The next part turns from the heritage to the inversion"). Before it comes an unanswered question (see 7).

## 6. Metaphors (C5)

- The legal frame (law, constitution, amendment, statute tier, outranks, court, verdict) is book-wide and carries weight. It is mapped elsewhere.
- "A list that admits no stranger" carries weight because it echoes the principle's "stranger". It is the only title that personifies.
- "the heritage to the inversion" uses the part names, so it is not decorative.
- "held" has unstable names: "an interface held by law" (bound) against "keeps the rules ... held" and "keeps their text held" (on hold). The same word does two jobs in one chapter.

## 7. Address (C6)

- I: about 8 uses (reading, outline, ruling, delegating, refusing).
- we: 0 outside a quote.
- you: 5 uses (the glossary's "If you open the book here", and the exercise).

There is one question, the last line before the bridge: "Should a sign be able to promise it?" Nothing on the page answers it, so it is rhetoric, or homework that is not marked as such.

There are no exclamations. The tool does not speak; the diagnostic messages are quoted as findings, which is correct.

## 8. Rhythm and noise (C7, §4)

The long sentence followed by a short verdict is present and strong: "Clean." comes 5 times and "Refused" 3 times, each after a run. The colon is used throughout, and dashes appear only inside record quotes.

- Intensifiers: 1 ("The specification says it plainly").
- Unbacked superlative: 1. "The field's oldest interface held by treaty is the road sign."
- Absolutes: "every language", "every other sign must conform", "never given again" (the last is backed).
- Calque: "I ruled the proposal" (French *statuer sur*).
- Vocabulary drift:
  - "The project's ledger keeps each **finding**": the book fixes "finding" as one entry of the verifier's report.
  - "The **kind of finding** states its invariant in the contract": the book's word is "diagnostic".
  - "Every other verb is **absent** there": the data's own term is "not at all".
- No forbidden words.

## 10. The stake (C10)

## 11. Most him and least him

Most him:
1. "Clean. A sign shows where the gate is; the gate's own control opens it. The line between a sign and a printed page is one verb."
2. "Read at its source, the treaty carries the line at this size. It is a closed vocabulary where it prescribes, and a system that every other sign must conform to."
3. "Refused under Rule 118 ... A sixth meaning is an amendment of the law, as a new sign is an amendment of the treaty."

Least him: "The court of this chapter, the fixtures of the suite that judge its claims, holds no declaration on signage. It proves the same move on the media and the list it holds. A promise that print cannot carry, refused, and its twin made lawful."

Reason: it opens on what is absent, its fragments have no verbs, and it leaves the chapter's proof in an apologetic key.

## 12. The one change

Rewrite The court in affirmative sentences that say what each pair of fixtures proves about a closed list (a verb a medium cannot carry, a medium outside the list, a meaning outside the five), and drop "holds no declaration on signage".

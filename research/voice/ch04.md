# Voice reading: chapter 4, The instruments of the field

Reader: a fresh voice reader (brief `writing-framework/briefs/voice-reader.md`), 2026-11-25. Nothing run, nothing changed.

## 1. Shape (C9)

Opening: three first-person sentences of roadmap ("In this chapter I take them one by one"); the first sentence says what the subject is. Kept.
Section closes: most hand over. Two close on a summary: *The field* ("So the field reads the criterion's target, the token's value…") and *Clean, and the drawing still owes* ("So Zayn holds two instruments, one for each object"). The chapter's close is a bridge ("the road sign"). Kept.
The `[AUTHOR: the stake…]` marker sits at the foot of *What I refused*, before *The court*; a stake reads as a section's close, so it sits better at the end of *The principle* or after *Where the field leads*.

## 2. Run, then read (C1)

*The law names its instruments* has no plate, run or figure: five rules and their `lint` heads, then an abstract paragraph. The worst one is: "That field is the bridge between a rule and the field's instruments. A rule states what a user must be able to do" (*The law names its instruments*). Second worst: the level-4 paragraph ("The contract, the file that binds each kind of finding to the law, holds…"), which piles three definitions and a quote of a file path into one stretch. *The principle* (Article II) puts the reading before the first run. The shape asks for that order, and the plate follows within a section.

## 3. The defect (C2)

*In your hands* is a real defect: a tile that has three findings and two lawful repairs, which "say different things". The closing question puts the choice in the reader's hands. In both pairs (checkout, cards) the wrong version comes before the right one. No correct code is rejected for a reason of taste. A wording point: the record notes "Two diagnostics for one defect is noise" (research §5). "Three defects stand in its few lines" can read as three findings that are each a defect. If the three findings answer two defects, say "three findings".

## 4. Where it stops (C3, AWF-08, AWF-14)

Lines that say what is not done or not held (public page):
- "The suite keeps no fixture for the painted page." (*What I refused*)
- "`LAYOUT_DISPLACEMENT` is not implemented and never will be here." (*What I refused*, a quote of a README)
- "The comparison claims no conformance to WCAG." / "do not cite WCAG, and this page does not claim they implement it" (*What I refused*)
- "one rule received none" / "a pointer to an instrument that does not exist" (*What I refused*)
- "the law has said nothing about that" (*Two cards drawn alike*)

Each can be said affirmatively: the suite proves the painted page through the audit's recorded runs; the verifier keeps to the declaration; the law asks what the user can do and WCAG keeps its own criteria.

Lines that read as underselling: "checks almost nothing a generator gets wrong" (*Where the field leads*, quoted) and "a much narrower set than axe-core's" (same). Both are sourced and stand beside the win. Even so, "almost nothing" is the strongest self-deprecation on the page. Say it at true size: the verifier reads intent, and generators err in the drawing.

Claims that sound larger than the page shows:
- "two interfaces that every instrument could pass" (opening): argued, not run against any instrument.
- "None of them holds the fact that a tap pays: no token, story, line of markup or pixel carries it" (*Selecting that pays*): an absolute about the whole field. An interaction test in a story could carry it.
- "no instrument that reads the drawn tile can tell them apart" (*In your hands*).
- "A project that adopts the law reads, rule by rule, which of its own instruments owes which check" (*The law names its instruments*): only the rules that carry `lint` say this.

*Where it stands* is present, affirmative and uses the stage words.

## 6. Metaphors (C5)

- The court: load-bearing, mapped at use ("the fixtures of the suite that judge its claims").
- The law: load-bearing, defined in the opening.
- "the ladder" / "moved up the ladder" / "enforcement ladder" (*Where it stands*, front matter, quote): not mapped on this page. A reader who opens the book here has no key to it.
- "the bridge between a rule and the field's instruments": decorative.
- "the drawing still owes" (title), "the drawing's business", "honest about its act", "The difference lives in one word": light, one job each.

## 7. Address (C6)

I: about 6 (the roadmap, "I moved", "I ruled"). We: 0. You: about 7 (the opening's "If you open the book here", the exercise's imperatives). Question: one ("Which one is true of your tile?"). It is the defect's question and is left to the reader on purpose. Exclamations: 0. That is under the profile, and a place has earned one after "Clean." in *Selecting that pays*. The tool speaks: "Refused: …" quotes its message, and "the command answers 0".

## 8. Rhythm and noise (C7, §4)

The long-then-short pattern is present ("Each instrument keeps its object. The declaration adds one."). Colons only, no dashes. Intensifiers: 0 in the prose ("just" only inside a quote). Superlatives: 0. Forbidden words: 0. Unbacked absolutes: 4 (see §4: "every instrument", "None of them", "no instrument", "never given again" is backed). The title "Selecting that pays" parses badly for a non-native reader ("a selection that pays"?).

## 9. Counted sets (C8)

The opening names six instruments: "a written criterion, a set of design tokens, a catalogue of components, a linter, an audit of the painted page, a visual test". *The field* treats a different six: it has "a measure of movement" where the opening has "an audit of the painted page". The summary line follows *The field*. The names must match. "four words come first": four. "Three defects": three promises (see §3). "Two repairs": two. The stage words are exact. The court paragraph names seven runs against seven fixtures.

## 10. The stake (C10)

The chapter rises at "None of them holds the fact that a tap pays" and at "Zayn leads in the moment… the verifier reads the intent before". The first is argued, not run. The second is backed by the comparison's quote. The people-level stake is held in the `[AUTHOR]` marker, as it should be.

## 11. Most him, least him

Most him:
- "Clean. One verb restored the boundary between interest and commitment, and no pixel of the drawing was named." (*Selecting that pays*)
- "The two cards are one drawing and two promises. The token reads the same value on both; the story renders the same component. The difference lives in one word of the declaration." (*Two cards drawn alike*)
- "The instruments of the field read the interface after it is drawn; the verifier reads the intent before." (*Where the field leads*)

Least him: "The contract, the file that binds each kind of finding to the law, holds {{n:diag.total}} kinds, and it places {{n:diag.level4}} of them at level 4" (*Clean, and the drawing still owes*). Two nested definitions, two tokens and a record's sentence about a file path. It is the record's voice, not a run read.

## 12. The one change

Rewrite the three absence items of *What I refused* (no fixture, "not implemented and never will be", no conformance claim) as affirmative statements of what each instrument holds, and bring the opening's six instruments in line with the six that *The field* reads.

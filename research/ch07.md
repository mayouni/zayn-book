## 1. What the chapter does

It gives the whole grammar of the language on one page: a declaration is `DEFINE <kind> <name> ( fields )`; a field is a
list of names or a key and a value; no declaration sits inside another; five fields take their value from a closed list.
It then shows each of the ten kinds once, in one lawful file, with one sentence on what each kind is for. It then makes the
grammar refuse: a field it does not know, a known field on a kind that does not carry it, a field or a declaration written
twice, a value outside a closed list, a declaration written inside another, and it shows what the verifier answers to a
kind outside the ten. It tells why the grammar is closed and flat, in the record's reasons. It follows chapter 6 (the 22
verbs, `SATISFIES`, `ALWAYS_REACHABLE`) and hands over to chapter 8 (the refusals that cite the law).

## 4. The principle

The sentence the record returns to, in three places and three subjects:

- "These lists are closed; extending them is a language change, not an implementation choice." (`doc/language/DECLARATIONS.md:281-282`)
- "a new medium is a language change, not an implementation choice" (`tools/zayn/zayn.js:97-98`)
- "adding a sixth status is a constitutional amendment, not an implementation choice" (`conformance/signal.json:15`)

with its reason in one clause: "a vocabulary that anyone may extend cannot be checked" (`conformance/violations.json:304`),
and its twin for the shape: "nesting is where widget trees creep back into an intent language" (`GRAMMAR.md:69-70`, repeated
in three other files).

Proposal for the chapter's one bold sentence, in the book's words (the stake is the author's, C10):

> **A declaration is made of two sorts of words: the names its author chooses, and the words the language already holds; the
> second sort is a closed list, and each word of it has one place.**

A shorter variant: **The grammar is closed and flat, so that every word of a declaration can be checked.** Both are true at
the size the runs show: a field, a value of the five lists, a verb, a second copy and a nested declaration are refused; a
kind outside the ten is warned of for two words and passed over for every other (finding F1), so the sentence should not
say that every unknown word is refused.

## 11. Terms a designer who does not program needs

| declaration | A few lines that say what a user is doing on a surface, never how it looks: `DEFINE <KIND> <name> ( fields )`. | `DECLARATIONS.md:10-16` |
| kind | The word after `DEFINE`: one of ten, each standing for one sort of thing a surface holds (a flow, a form, a screen, a control…). | `GRAMMAR.md:43-46` |
| name | The word the author chooses for a declaration; unique among declarations of its kind. | `GRAMMAR.md:22`, `:82-84` |
| field | One line of a declaration's body: a key followed by a list of names, or by one value. | `GRAMMAR.md:50-64` |
| list field | A field whose value is names between square brackets (`SATISFIES [SCAN, COMPARE]`). | `GRAMMAR.md:52-57` |
| value field | A field whose value is one word, or one text between quotation marks (`TONE critical`, `LABEL "Payments"`). | `GRAMMAR.md:59-64` |
| closed list (enumeration) | A field's complete set of allowed values; a word outside it is refused, and adding a word changes the language. | `DECLARATIONS.md:229-240`, `:281-282` |
| grammar | The rules that say which sequences of words are a declaration; here stated on one page, in a notation named EBNF. | `GRAMMAR.md:3-8`, `:39-64` |

| nesting | Writing one thing inside another; the grammar has none. | `GRAMMAR.md:67-70` |
| keyword | A word of the language, written in capitals (`DEFINE`, `SCREEN`, `SATISFIES`); capitals matter. | `GRAMMAR.md:20-21`, `:32-34` |
| comment | Text from `//` to the end of a line; the verifier skips it. | `GRAMMAR.md:17-18` |

| level | How much a verifier must see to decide a finding: level 1 the grammar of the file, level 2 one declaration's meaning. | `README.md:124-129`; `violations.json:10-15` |

## 1. What the chapter does

It says what the subject is: UI governance, the governance of interfaces and nothing wider, held by one law, Zayn's interface
constitution. It shows the deterioration the book answers: AI models draw interfaces fast and reproduce the conventions of what they
learned from; a person corrects them one remark at a time, and the same defect comes back. It sets the book's three moves before the
reader, each shown once: declare what a user is doing (the declaration); hold the declaration to a law kept as data (the verifier, the
audit of the painted page, a person); keep the corrections so that they become rules. It ends on the reader's first act: a real
first draft, refused, and two repairs the verifier accepts, of which one is true.

## 4. The principle

The record returns to one sentence in three forms, each with its path:

- `site/case.html:105-106`: "The only correction that keeps pace with a generator is a rule that the generator can be made to check."
- `README.md:13`: "Without a machine-checkable law, the defects regenerate endlessly."
- `site/why.html:135`: "A correction stored as a symptom moves the defect to the next page; a rule stated as its cause, bounded on
  both sides and checked on the painted page, ends it."

Proposals for the bold sentence, in the book's words (the stake of the chapter is the author's, C10; these are proposals):

- **"A remark corrects one page, once; a rule kept as data is checked on every declaration an instrument reads."**
- **"To govern an interface is to write down what it owes its user, once, as a rule that an instrument can check."**

The first keeps the true size in its last words: the rule holds where an instrument reads it.

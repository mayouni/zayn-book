## 1. What the chapter does

It states the inversion on which Zayn rests: an interface is declared from what its user is doing, and a widget is what an
intent becomes in a medium, never the unit a declaration starts from. It is the first chapter where a reader meets a
declaration, so it introduces `DEFINE`, `SATISFIES` and `ALWAYS_REACHABLE` from nothing, for a designer who does not program.
It gives the verbs in their six families, each with its invariant, and shows that the set is closed: a word outside it gets
`UNKNOWN_INTENT`, `NAVIGATE` first among them. It shows a flow that names no intent (`INTENT_FLOW_NO_SATISFIES`), an element
that traces to no intent (`INTENT_ORPHAN`, at level 3, against an inventory supplied as a symbol table) and `DEFINE WIDGET`.
It says why starting from intent makes an interface checkable before anything is drawn, at the size the runs support.

## 13. Hazards

- **"Refuses" is true of two of the four kinds of finding the blocks show.** `UNKNOWN_INTENT` and `INTENT_ORPHAN` are
  errors (so is the court's `EXPRESSION_ORPHAN`). `INTENT_FLOW_NO_SATISFIES` and `UNSPECIFIED_DECLARATION` are warnings and
  exit 0: by the book's vocabulary they are findings, never refusals. "The language refuses a widget" is not what the run
  shows; "the language has no kind for a widget, and reports the word" is.
- **Level.** `INTENT_ORPHAN` is never said to be decided from the file. No sentence of the form "every element is checked"
  without "at level 3, against an inventory the verifier is given".
- **"Before anything is drawn"** is the site's phrase and is true of what a declaration states. The principle must not
  claim the correctness of the whole interface.
- **No tier count.** Rule 130's `enforcement` is `machine`: that rule's own word, and the `{{rule:130}}` card prints it.
  Nothing about how many rules a machine refuses. The card prints the rule's `why` and not its `evidence`
  (`tools/build_book.py:656-671`); the evidence names the coordinating session and the author's title, and stays off the page.
- **Counts are tokens.** `{{n:law.verbs}}`, `{{n:law.families}}`, `{{n:law.family.action}}`, `{{n:lang.kinds}}` exist
  (`tools/book_common.py:193-210`). The other five families have no key and there is no card that prints a family with its
  verbs and its invariant from the law's data: the chapter needs both from the book's own tools before it prints the
  families, or it types a count and a law's sentence. The verifier's own report prints "the 22"; that is the run's text.
- **Names the book does not use, met in this chapter's sources.** The language's former names stand in
  `doc/language/README.md:18-33` and `:98`, in the title and body of `doc/ARCHITECTURE.md`, in `constitution/rules.json:59`

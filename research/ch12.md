## 1. What the chapter does

It takes one declaration, written once with no channel named, and checks it under several values of `SURFACE_AS`, the field of a
`VIBE` that names the channel (the record's word: the medium) a surface is drawn for. Each run is printed: the lamp and the
wristband refuse what the screen, the terminal, the voice line and the program accept. It shows that each of the ten channels is a
row of data in `conformance/media.json`, described by six properties and by what it does with each of the 22 verbs (carries it
natively, degraded, or not at all), and that the verifier reads that row to refuse a promise the declared channel cannot carry
(`VERB_NOT_CARRIED`), a status that the channel renders as nothing (`SIGNAL_UNPERCEIVABLE`), and a channel outside the ten
(`UNKNOWN_MEDIUM`). It says what the cross-medium instrument asserts, how many of the ten channels have a renderer on record, and
what the data holds for the `machine` channel, where the user is a program or an AI agent (the bridge to Part IX).

## 4. The principle

The record returns to one sentence, in the contract and in the specification:

> "A new medium adds renderings, never verbs." (`conformance/violations.json:290`, the invariant of `VERB_NOT_CARRIED`;
> the same at `:148` and `doc/language/README.md:78`)

and to its pair in the design: "A medium declares which of the 22 verbs it carries, and at what cost."
(`doc/THE-SECOND-FOUNDING.md:309`, designed, not ratified).

Proposed bold sentence, in the book's words (the author's to accept): **A declaration is written once, for every channel; the
channel it names decides which of its promises can be kept, and the verifier refuses the ones that channel cannot keep at all.**

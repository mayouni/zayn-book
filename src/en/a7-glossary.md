This appendix gives each term of the book in one or two sentences, in the words the chapters use, with the chapter that says it first or most fully. A term in `code` is a word of the language or of its data.

**AI agent.** A program, driven by an AI model, that acts for a user; to the law it is a user like a person. Chapters 22 and 23.

**AI model.** A program that generates text, code or an interface from what it is given; an AI model draws many of the interfaces the law reads. Chapter 22.

**Article.** One of the law's {{n:law.articles}} statements of principle; every rule derives from one. The article on a finding's line is the one its kind of finding enforces. Chapter 16.

**Audit of the painted page.** A page of Zayn's repository that opens another page in a browser and measures what is drawn. Chapters 10 and 17, Appendix D.

**Built, designed, next.** The book's three stage words. Built: it ran on a page or runs in Zayn's repository at the release the book reads. Designed: it is specified, and its build comes after. Next: the direction.

**Check.** One of the numbered measures of Zayn's design system, K01 to K37, each reading one property of every element that has it; {{n:audit.paint}} read the painted page and {{n:audit.files}} the site's files. Chapter 10, Appendix D.

**Commons, core, local.** Three of the four tiers of law that a proposal of the project designs, under the statutes and binding standards above the law: the core every adopter keeps, the commons many teams contribute to, and a team's own local law; a local law may be stricter, never looser. Chapter 21.

**Contract.** The file that binds each kind of finding to its level, its severity and what it cites in the law. Chapter 8, Appendix C.

**Corpus of corrections.** The author's remarks on interfaces, kept as data, each with its words, its date, its cause and the decision it led to. Chapters 2 and 20.

**Court.** The fixtures of Zayn's suite that judge a chapter's claims, run at each build of the book. Chapter 8.

**Culture of interaction, vibe.** The way one interface presents itself to a community of users; declared as a `VIBE`, it may change the look and never take away what a declaration keeps within reach. Chapter 14.

**Declaration.** A few lines of Zayn that say what a user is doing on a surface, and say nothing of shape or colour. Chapters 1, 6 and 7.

**Diagnostic.** A kind of finding in the contract, with its code, such as `MISSING_UNDO`. Appendix C.

**Docket.** The file of Zayn's repository where changes to the law are proposed and ruled. Chapters 16 and 18.

**Drawing.** What a medium shows of a declaration: a screen, a voice, a printed page. Chapters 1 and 10.

**Exit code.** The number a command returns when it ends: 0 when the file passes, 1 when it is refused, 2 when it cannot be read. Chapters 8 and 25.

**Finding, refusal.** A finding is one entry of the verifier's report; a refusal is a finding that is an error. Chapters 1 and 8.

**Fixture.** A declaration kept in Zayn's suite with the answer it must get. Chapter 8.

**Forbidden pattern.** A named defect the law forbids, bound to the rules it violates. Chapter 16.

**Grid.** The media crossed with the activities of the user; a cell is closed, holds rules, or waits for one. Chapter 13, Appendix E.

**Intent, verb.** What a user is trying to do, named by one of the law's {{n:law.verbs}} verbs, such as `SELECT`, `ACT` or `UNDO`. Chapter 6, Appendix B.

**Lawful twin.** The lawful version of a refused declaration, kept beside it in the suite or on a page. Chapter 8.

**Law.** Zayn's constitution of {{n:law.rules}} rules for interfaces, kept as a data file and pinned by its version. Chapter 16, Appendix A.

**Ledger.** The dated list of what the project found, each entry with its evidence and its status. Chapter 2.

**Level.** A stage of what a check needs to read: level 1 the file's grammar and words, level 2 the file alone, level 3 a table of what the system knows, level 4 a painted page. Chapter 9, Appendix B.

**Manifest.** The pack's list of its files, each with its hash, against which the pack's check compares the files a team holds. Chapter 25.

**Machine form.** The verifier's report as data a tool reads, each finding with its code and what it cites. Chapter 8.

**Medium.** A channel that carries an interface: visual, auditory, voice, haptic, print, luminous, signage, machine, spatial or terminal. Chapter 12.

**Modern, Retro.** The Zayn site's two cultures of interaction, named `curator` and `guardian` in the law's notes. Chapter 14.

**Operator.** The law's word for its user, a person or an AI agent.

**Painted page.** The drawing of an interface in a browser.

**Pin, pack, hash.** A team pins the version of the law it is held to; the pack is the {{n:pack.files}} files a team pins, among them the law, the language's specification, the contract and the suite's list of fixtures, each recorded by its hash, a number computed from a file's bytes that changes if a byte changes. Chapters 18, 21 and 25.

**Plate.** The book's figure: a declaration, the line "In words", and the verifier's answer as the run gave it.

**Reading, convention.** What a sign means to the person who receives it; a proposal of the project calls the reader's culture an axis and proposes the word `CONVENTION` for it. Chapter 15.

**Record.** The files of Zayn's repository at the release the book reads.

**Right.** One of the {{n:law.rights}} rights the law gives its user: to understand, to reverse, to know, to leave, to act without fear and to reach a human. Chapter 16.

**Role.** A control's promise about what it changes: `inert`, `navigate`, `reversible`, `irreversible` or `proposal`. Chapters 4 and 11.

**Rule.** One numbered demand of the law, with its title, its text, what it avoids, its reason, the article it derives from and its tier. Chapters 16 and 19.

**Second Founding, The.** A proposal of the project for a new base of the law, which awaits the author's ruling section by section. Chapters 3 and 11.

**Set.** A group of the law's rules on one subject. Chapter 16.

**Stratum.** One function of expression between what a user does and how a medium draws it: structure, signal, role, motion, message or tempo. Chapter 11.

**Symbol table.** A small file that tells the verifier what the system holds, its entities, actors and states; a check of level 3 needs one. Chapter 9.

**Tier.** Two senses. A rule's tier is the law's word for what is owed to check it: `judgment`, `machine-later`, `tool` or `machine` (chapter 17). The tiers of law, statute, core, commons and local, are the levels at which a rule is kept (chapter 21).

**Verifier.** The program that checks a declaration against the law and prints its findings. Chapters 1, 8 and 9.

**Widget.** A drawn control: a button, a card, a tile. A declaration names no widget. Chapter 4.

**Zayn.** The language of this book, in which an interface is declared and held to its law.
